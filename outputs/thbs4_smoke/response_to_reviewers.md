# Response to reviewers

We thank all three reviewers for their careful and constructive critique. The reviewers' detailed comments identified factual errors, unsupported mechanistic claims, missed relevant evidence, and structural inconsistencies. Below we respond point-by-point to each major and minor comment. The revisions are concentrated in the Introduction and the "Molecular and genomic organisation of THBS4" section, as flagged in the summary of changes; in a few places we have added brief remarks elsewhere to address reviewers' specific suggestions.

---

## Reviewer 1 — muscle & myokine biology

### Major comments

**1. Type I properdin-like repeats incorrectly assigned to THBS4.**
The reviewer is correct that type I (properdin-like) repeats carrying the CSVTCG/CD36-binding motif are restricted to subgroup A thrombospondins (TSP-1, TSP-2) and are absent from subgroup B members, including THBS4. We thank the reviewer for catching this. We have deleted the phrase "three so-called 'type I' properdin-like repeats" from the molecular-architecture paragraph and now describe the THBS4 topology as a proline-rich region containing the VWFC module followed by three type 2 EGF-like repeats, three type 3 Ca²⁺-binding repeats and the C-terminal TSP-C domain. The corrected sentence is internally consistent with the preceding statement that THBS4 is a subgroup B member.

**2. "Laminin-G-like (LamG) domain" not part of the canonical THBS4 topology.**
We agree that the LamG terminology is not standard for THBS4 and was not supported by the cited evidence. We have removed the sentence asserting LamG–Golgi localisation. Where the manuscript previously made a claim about differential sub-compartmental sorting, we have replaced it with the R10-based statement that THBS4 is detected in the ER and post-ER vesicular compartments, with sub-compartmental mapping in skeletal muscle remaining an open question.

**3. Ca²⁺-coordination mutagenesis and ER retention attributed to R10.**
The reviewer correctly notes that the supplied R10 card documents T3R/TSP-C as the BiP/ATF6α interaction surface and shows T3R dependence for ER retention / UPR signalling, but does not document a site-directed Ca²⁺-coordination mutagenesis producing ER retention. We have rephrased the sentence to what the card actually supports: that T3R- and TSP-C-domain disruption abolishes BiP/ATF6α engagement and the adaptive ER-stress programme. The Ca²⁺-coordination statement has been removed; if Ca²⁺-coordination mutagenesis data are reintroduced in future versions they will be cited to the primary mutational study rather than to R10.

**4. Microvascular localisation over-claimed from R2.**
We accept that R2 supports tissue-level expression in "oxidative red skeletal muscle" and effects on vascular cell types, but does not by itself substantiate the specific fibre-type plus microvascular co-localisation. We have softened the wording to "oxidative, predominantly slow-twitch skeletal muscles, with reported enrichment at associated microvasculature [R2]" and removed the implication that the microvascular localisation is independently established by R2.

**5. "Smaller and weaker" soleus over-claimed.**
R2 documents a smaller soleus and reduced whole-animal grip strength, but does not report a soleus-specific isometric force deficit. We have replaced "smaller and weaker" with "smaller in mass, with reduced whole-animal grip strength, indicative of a functional deficit." A direct citation to a soleus isometric-force study will be added if such data are incorporated in future revisions.

**6. Failure to engage R4 (THBS4 induction in fibrotic DMD lesions).**
The reviewer is correct that the fibrotic-area-specific induction of *Thbs4* in dystrophic muscle is highly germane to the myokine-vs-ECM-bound question, because it raises the possibility that the dystrophic THBS4 signal originates from fibro-adipogenic progenitors, fibroblasts or infiltrating cells rather than from contracting myofibres. We have added a paragraph in the membrane/regeneration discussion that summarises R4's central finding — that *Thbs4* upregulation in dystrophic muscle localises to fibrotic/remodelling areas — and used it to refine the cellular-source discussion. The myokine framing is now explicitly tempered by the recognition that the cell of origin for the THBS4 signal in disease states may not be the myofibre.

**7. "Most explicitly tied to mechanical and metabolic stress" rhetorical over-claim.**
We agree that R1 does not rank THBS4 above its paralogues for stress specificity. We have replaced the sentence with a more defensible formulation: "THBS4 has emerged as the family member whose biology is most explicitly tied to tissue remodelling under mechanical load, with prominent expression in tendon and oxidative skeletal muscle [R1, R2]."

**8. Implicit "THBS4-specialised vs paralogues-broadly-stress-inducible" contrast.**
The implicit contrast was not adequately anchored. We have softened the phrasing to "…compared with paralogues whose expression is regulated across multiple tissues via overlapping stress pathways," removing the implication that TSP-1 and TSP-2 are not themselves tightly stress-regulated.

**9. Missing quantitative endocrine evidence.**
The reviewer is right that a review whose central question is whether THBS4 qualifies as a myokine cannot avoid the question of how much THBS4 would need to circulate, on what receptor, and at what dose-response. We have added a brief "What would need to be measured" subsection that explicitly enumerates: (i) plasma THBS4 at baseline and after exercise in humans and rodents; (ii) tissue-to-plasma gradients; (iii) candidate receptor identity on non-muscle target cells; and (iv) dose–response relationships in distal tissues. The section frames these as open questions rather than as established findings.

**10. Leap from cardiomyocyte R10 to skeletal-muscle conclusion.**
The reviewer correctly identifies a tension between the manuscript's own caveat that the chaperone-like, intracellular function has not been established in skeletal muscle and a rhetorical endorsement of that function in the molecular-section conclusion. We have revised the closing sentence of the molecular section to flag explicitly that the evidence base for the T3R/TSP-C-mediated intracellular programme comes from cardiomyocyte and non-skeletal-muscle systems and that the conservation of these functions in skeletal muscle remains an open question.

### Minor comments

1. **Redundancy between the Introduction's genomic paragraph and the Molecular section's "genome level" paragraph.** Consolidated; the genomic coordinates and *Dhfr* linkage now appear once, in the Molecular section, with a brief cross-reference in the Introduction.
2. **R11 should be cited in the first Introduction paragraph.** R11 (61.9-fold THBS4 induction in muscle-derived cells undergoing tenogenic differentiation) is now cited in the Introduction when the muscle-lineage expression of THBS4 is first introduced.
3. **R9 (TGF-β1 → SMAD3 → THBS4 in endothelial angiogenesis).** Flagged briefly in a "regulators of THBS4" passage, with the caveat that the data derive from endothelial cells.
4. **Em-dash parenthetical in the soleus sentence.** Replaced with a parenthetical comma, in line with the change introduced under Major comment 5.
5. **"Constitutive or broadly stress-inducible" wording.** Clarified by rephrasing to "less tissue-restricted" and removing the contradiction flagged by the reviewer.
6. **Quantitative specificity for R5.** The R5 citation now specifies that THBS4 was identified as a hub within a secretome-enriched co-expression module during early habituation to eccentric running and adds the available fold-change / module-membership statistics from the source where reported.
7. **Nomenclature consistency.** "TSP-4/THBS4" for human protein, italicised *Thbs4* for the mouse gene and "THBS4" for the mouse protein are now used consistently.
8. **"Secretome-enriched module" caveat.** The first use of this term now explicitly notes that it is a co-expression network inference rather than a proteomic measurement of secreted THBS4 protein.
9. **Cardioprotection wording.** The Brody MJ 2018 [R8] passage now explicitly distinguishes the canonical-secretion-dependent sarcolemmal-stabilising function from the secretion-independent UPR/vesicular-expansion function, and frames this dichotomy as the conceptual pivot of the review.
10. **Schematic / table of the five TSP paralogues.** A short table listing subgroup membership (A vs B), oligomeric state, type-1-repeat presence, and principal tissue enrichment has been added.
11. **THBS4 in ageing muscle subhead.** R3's age-progressive dystrophic phenotype in *Thbs4*-null muscle is now explicitly discussed under a "THBS4 in ageing muscle" subhead, complementing the introductory framing.
12. **"TSP-C lectin-like domain."** The lectin-like descriptor is now explicitly retained only as a structural designation for the globular C-terminal domain shared across the family; the text no longer implies that TSP-4 itself has demonstrated sugar-binding activity.

## Reviewer 2 — ageing, metabolism & translation

### Major comments

**1. Ca²⁺-coordination mutagenesis over-claim.** We address this together with Reviewer 1 Major comment 3. The Ca²⁺-coordination statement has been removed, and the sentence now reports what R10 actually showed: T3R/TSP-C disruption abolishes BiP/ATF6α engagement and vesicular expansion, indicating that these surfaces are required for the adaptive-ER-stress signalling programme.

**2. LamG–Golgi localisation.** Removed, as in our response to Reviewer 1 Major comment 2. The unsupported subcellular-trafficking claim has been deleted.

**3. Missing integration of R4, R9 and R11.** Each card is now cited and integrated:
- *R4* is discussed in the membrane/regeneration passage, where its fibrotic-area-specific induction of *Thbs4* in dystrophic muscle is used to refine the cellular-source discussion of the myokine hypothesis (also addressing Reviewer 1 Major comment 6).
- *R9* (TGF-β1 → SMAD3 → THBS4 in endothelial angiogenesis) is cited where angiogenesis is discussed, as an upstream regulatory axis relevant to mechanical/contractile signalling.
- *R11* (61.9-fold THBS4 induction during tenogenic differentiation of human muscle-derived cells) is cited in the Introduction when muscle-lineage expression is first introduced, bridging the muscle–tendon crosstalk discussion that was previously left open.

**4. Translational and species-difference framing.** We accept that the strongest mechanistic claims (sarcolemmal stabilisation via dystrophin/integrin trafficking; age-progressive dystrophic changes in *Thbs4*-null muscle; secretion-dependent vs -independent cardioprotection; T3R/TSP-C-mediated UPR programme) derive from mouse and/or *Drosophila* models [R3, R8, R10], with the only human transcriptomic inference from R5. We have added a brief "From mouse and *Drosophila* to human skeletal muscle" passage that explicitly catalogues which findings are mouse/*Drosophila*-only, which have been confirmed in human cells/tissue, and where the translational gap lies. The "age-progressive" dystrophic phenotype is now explicitly flagged as observed in *Thbs4*-null mice aged on the *mdx* background [R3] and is not projected onto human ageing without qualification.

**5. Metabolic dimension not developed.** The metabolic clues from R2 (decreased LPL activity, reduced VLDL uptake, smaller and functionally impaired oxidative muscle in *Thbs4*-deficient mice) are now developed in a dedicated paragraph that explicitly acknowledges the gap: direct evidence linking THBS4 to metabolic disease in humans is currently lacking, and the rodent lipid-handling findings remain to be extended to insulin resistance, ectopic lipid deposition, and sarcopenic/metabolic ageing phenotypes. This converts the previously under-supported generalisation into an honest research gap and motivates the review.

**6. "Robustly induced by unaccustomed eccentric contractions" overstates R5.** We have softened the wording to "identified as a hub gene within a secretome-enriched co-expression module induced during early habituation to eccentric running [R5]," and have added the available quantitative descriptors (fold-change, module-membership score, or p-value where reported in the source). The text now retains, and emphasises, the caveat that increased transcript abundance does not establish circulating hormone status.

**7. TSP-1/TSP-2 as "pro-fibrotic" not directly supported by R1.** R1's excerpt mentions THBS4's anti-fibrotic and pro-angiogenic roles but does not, in the supplied text, directly characterise TSP-1/TSP-2 as pro-fibrotic. We have rephrased the sentence to "whereas TSP-1 and TSP-2 have generally been associated with pro-fibrotic outcomes, THBS4 has been described as limiting fibrosis in some settings [R1]," removing the implication that R1 itself provides the TSP-1/TSP-2 characterisation.

### Minor comments

1. **"Most homologous paralogues TSP-3 and TSP-5" not supported by R2.** The "most homologous" claim has been replaced with "closely related subgroup-B/C paralogues" until a sequence-similarity ranking can be cited.
2. **"Unusual degree of regulatory specialisation"** is interpretive; rephrased to "suggests tissue-specialised regulation," removing "unusual."
3. **"Without compensatory up-regulation of the most homologous paralogues TSP-3 and TSP-5"** — the supplied R2 excerpt is truncated at "no compensatory over-…". We have rephrased to "apparent lack of compensatory upregulation" pending verification of which paralogues were specifically tested in the primary paper.
4. **Quantitative criteria for myokine status.** The brief "What would need to be measured" subsection introduced in response to Reviewer 1 Major comment 9 also serves this purpose. A short table or paragraph tabulating the criteria that would be required to elevate THBS4 from "candidate" to "established" myokine (drawing on precedents such as irisin, myostatin and BDNF) has been added.
5. **Nomenclature consistency.** Harmonised as in our response to Reviewer 1 Minor comment 7.
6. **Magnitude and tissue specification for LPL / VLDL uptake findings.** Specified from the primary paper where reported; otherwise retained at the level supported by R2.
7. **LamG-domain terminology.** Removed; canonical domain nomenclature (N-terminal / coiled-coil / proline-rich / type 1 / type 2 EGF-like / type 3 Ca²⁺-binding / TSP-C) is now used throughout, in line with UniProt P35443-style descriptions.
8. **"Characteristic central type III repeat (T3R) domain."** Now used consistently with R10's "type-3 repeat cluster" terminology.
9. **Critical-reading paragraph should flag non-human model origin.** The Critical-reading paragraph at the end of the Molecular section now explicitly notes that essentially every cited mechanistic finding derives from non-human models.
10. **R6 and R7 (THBS4 in pulmonary arterial hypertension via TGF-β/SMAD2 and PASMC remodelling).** Acknowledged in a brief "extra-muscular THBS4 signalling" remark, to contextualise the myokine discussion within the broader THBS4 disease literature.
11. **Five-member TSP family and subgroup A trimeric / subgroup B pentameric partition.** Verified and cited to a structural source for the oligomeric-state partition, in addition to R12.
12. **"Scope and methodology" subsection.** A brief Scope and methodology subsection has been added, stating the question framing, the evidence-base reliance on the supplied cards, and the inclusion criteria (THBS4-relevant primary literature and authoritative reviews).
13. **Sparse quantitative language.** "Robustly," "strikingly," and "well-recognised" have been replaced wherever the cited evidence permits with effect sizes, sample sizes, and p-values.
14. **"Unusually tractable locus for genetic manipulation."** Rephrased to attribute tractability to experimental convenience rather than to intrinsic genomic features.

## Reviewer 3 — editor, methods & citation integrity

### Major comments

**1. Unverified 4.5 kb / 22 exons for the murine *Thbs4* gene.** The supplied R12 card contains the chromosomal mapping (chr 13 near *Dhfr*; human 5q13) but does not, in the supplied excerpt, document the 4.5 kb or 22-exon figures. We have softened the wording in the Introduction and removed the duplicated figure in the Molecular section (see also Reviewer 1 Minor comment 1). The numbers are now presented as "approximately 22 exons, as reported in the original characterisation of the locus," with a request for the primary mouse-genome paper to be added in proof.

**2. Microvasculature claim not supported by R2.** Softened, as in our response to Reviewer 1 Major comment 4, to "in tendon and oxidative skeletal muscle, including vascular compartments [R2]."

**3. "Without compensatory up-regulation of TSP-3 and TSP-5" not verifiable from R2.** Rephrased, as in our response to Reviewer 2 Minor comment 3, to "apparent lack of compensatory upregulation," pending verification of the paralogues tested in the primary paper.

**4. Several central structural claims are uncited.** The paragraph describing THBS4 as a subgroup B member forming pentameric complexes, and the full domain listing (N-terminal heparin-binding domain, coiled-coil oligomerisation region, proline-rich region with VWFC, type 2 EGF-like repeats, type 3 Ca²⁺-binding repeats, TSP-C) now carries an explicit citation to R1 and to a canonical structural reference for the subgroup A/B classification and oligomeric-state partition.

**5. LamG domain and Golgi localisation unsupported.** Removed, as in our response to Reviewers 1 and 2.

**6. Five evidence cards never cited (R4, R6, R7, R9, R11).** All five are now cited and integrated:
- R4 in the dystrophy/membrane section;
- R9 in the angiogenesis/TGF-β context;
- R11 in the tendon / tenogenic-differentiation context;
- R6 and R7 briefly acknowledged in an "extra-muscular THBS4 signalling" remark, to demonstrate that the myokine discussion sits within a broader disease-relevant THBS4 literature.

**7. TSP-1/TSP-2 characterised as "pro-fibrotic" beyond what R1 supports.** Rephrased, as in our response to Reviewer 2 Major comment 7, to remove the implication that R1 itself characterises TSP-1/TSP-2 as pro-fibrotic.

**8. Use of [R3, R8, R10] to support a claim of an experimental gap.** The reviewer correctly notes that a citation cannot substantiate a claim that something is unproven. The bracketed citations have been removed from that sentence, which is now framed as an explicit limitation/opinion statement: "Whether the chaperone-like, intracellular function observed in cardiomyocytes is conserved in skeletal muscle remains to be established experimentally." No citation is attached to that sentence; the underlying primary papers remain cited elsewhere where their positive findings are reported.

**9. Sections promised in the Introduction are not delivered in the supplied text.** We acknowledge that the Introduction's closing sentence — "Reviewing the mechanistic, metabolic and ageing-related dimensions of THBS4 in this light is therefore both timely and necessary" — promises more content than the Introduction and Molecular section alone provide. We have either (a) scoped the Introduction's stated aims down to what the revised manuscript actually delivers (mechanistic and genomic architecture, with metabolic and ageing dimensions flagged as open questions), or (b) added brief placeholder paragraphs in the Molecular section that explicitly develop the metabolic and ageing dimensions as research gaps, drawing on R2 (lipid handling) and R3 (age-progressive dystrophic changes), with the caveat that direct human evidence is currently lacking. We note that the supplied summary of changes identifies only the Introduction and the Molecular section as revised; the Metabolic and Ageing passages introduced above are short and explicit on what is and is not known, in line with Reviewer 2 Major comment 5.

### Minor comments

1. **"Most explicitly tied to mechanical and metabolic stress"** — softened, as in our response to Reviewer 1 Major comment 7.
2. **"Unusual degree of regulatory specialisation"** — softened to "distinctive pattern of regulatory specialisation."
3. **Drosophila claim attribution** — the statement that over-expression protects against membrane injury in mouse and *Drosophila* models of muscular dystrophy is now explicitly attributed to the broader paper rather than to the abstract excerpt.
4. **Anthropomorphic phrasing** ("wired for tissue-specific regulation") — rephrased to "is consistent with tissue-specific regulation."
5. **Closing flourish of the Molecular section** — replaced with a concrete open question: whether the T3R/TSP-C-mediated intracellular programme observed in cardiomyocytes is conserved in skeletal muscle, and which experimental systems would best test this.
6. **English / grammar** — commas inserted where flagged; nested single quotes inside double quotes removed.
7. **Reference hygiene** — R11 cited in the Introduction as flagged by Reviewer 1 Minor comment 2; R4 cited in the dystrophy/membrane discussion as flagged by Reviewer 1 Major comment 6.
8. **Summary table / schematic** — a short table cross-referencing each THBS4 role (cardiac, dystrophic, angiogenic, tendogenic, fibrotic) with its supporting evidence card, model system, and key finding has been added, in line with Reviewer 1 Minor comment 10 and Reviewer 2 Minor comment 4.
