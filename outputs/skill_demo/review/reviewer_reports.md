# Reviewer 1 — bioinformatics methods & software engineering

```markdown
## Summary assessment

This manuscript attempts an ambitious comparative survey of multi-omics integration software with EasyMultiProfiler (EMP) as the focal case study, but it cannot be accepted in its present form. The most consequential problem is that several specific behavioural claims about EMP — most prominently the "natural-language-style interface" and the assertion that it draws on timeOmics-, netOmics- and metaQuantome-style components — are not supported by the only source allowed (R1), and some of these claims directly contradict other claims made in the same manuscript. The text also contains duplicate references (R11/R18 and R29/R30 are the same paper), at least one quoted phrase attributed to R26 that does not appear in that card, an unfair framing of MOFA+/SNF as "single integration algorithms" against which a workflow is naturally favoured, and notable omissions in the tool census. **Recommendation: major revision required.**

## Major comments

1. **The "natural-language-style interface" claim about EMP is not supported by R1.** In Section "EasyMultiProfiler (EMP): design, strengths and limitations" the manuscript states that the five modules are "invoked through a natural-language-style interface so that the user does not have to manage incompatibilities between upstream taxonomic profilers and downstream statistical engines," and later repeats the "natural-language-style interface" formulation. The R1 evidence card contains only the abstract excerpt and key finding, neither of which mentions any natural-language syntax. Either remove these characterisations or supply a primary citation from R1 (full text) that substantiates them; in a card-only review they must be excised.

2. **Internal contradiction regarding EMP's longitudinal capability.** In Section "Microbiome-centred multi-omics tools" the manuscript claims EMP supports "timeOmics-style longitudinal modelling [R36]" as one of its components; later in Section "Data structures, workflow systems and reproducibility infrastructure" the same paper concedes that "EMP, focused on microbiome research, does not currently advertise time-aware wrappers comparable to timeOmics." These two statements cannot both stand. If the second is correct, the first must be softened to "potential compatibility with" or removed; if the first is correct, the second must explain why a user would not see advertised wrappers for a feature the workflow in fact provides.

3. **Specific claims about EMP's algorithmic inclusions go beyond R1.** The sentence "The five-module design accommodates taxonomic and functional profiling, differential abundance, network analysis, time-series modelling and visualisation, drawing on — rather than re-implementing — components such as mixOmics-based PLS/DIABLO integration [R3, R44], timeOmics-style longitudinal modelling [R36], netOmics-style network interpretation [R37] and metaQuantome-style metaproteomic and metatranscriptomic quantitative analysis [R42]" lists four specific external tools that EMP is said to wrap. The R1 card documents only the five generic module names and the SummarizedExperiment/MultiAssayExperiment substrate; it does not enumerate which third-party packages are bundled. Reword to "is compatible in principle with" or cite R1's full text; do not present these inclusions as facts derivable from the allowed evidence.

4. **Specific claim that EMP treats "16S, metagenomic, metatranscriptomic, metaproteomic, and host-transcriptomic layers as first-class assays" goes beyond R1.** R1's key finding supports only "a unified, reproducible multi-omics data integration framework for microbiome research." Layer-level claims should be either moved into a clearly tentative paragraph ("if the framework's input contracts accept these layers, then …") or backed by full-text evidence from R1.

5. **Duplicate references inflate the citation count.** R11 (Ramos et al., Bioinformatics 2026) and R18 (Ramos et al., ArXiv 2025) are the same BioNeuralNet tool described twice in the same paragraph; similarly R29 (Wieder et al., bioRxiv 2024) and R30 (Wieder et al., PLoS Comput Biol 2024) are the preprint and journal versions of the same PathIntegrate paper. Consolidate to the peer-reviewed version in each case (R11/R18 → R11; R29/R30 → R30) and remove the redundant entries, otherwise the review appears to draw on twice as many primary sources as it actually does.

6. **Misattributed direct quote from R26.** The manuscript writes that the Hu et al. benchmark "found that 'overall totalVI and scArches outperformed' the field but with substantial dataset-dependent variability [R26]" and shortly afterwards lists "Seurat, MOJITOO and scAI/UINMF-family methods" as top performers. The R26 card enumerates six top performers (totalVI, scArches, LS_Lab, Seurat, MOJITOO, UINMF), does not single out only two as outperforming the field, and does not mention scAI. Replace the quoted phrase with a paraphrase that the card supports (e.g., "identified totalVI, scArches, LS_Lab, Seurat, MOJITOO and UINMF as top performers"), and drop scAI unless an additional source can be cited.

7. **Unfair framing of MOFA+ and SNF as "single integration algorithms" to elevate EMP.** The taxonomy sections repeatedly contrast EMP with "a single integration algorithm such as MOFA+ [R4] or SNF [R5]," implying that wrapping algorithms is intrinsically more valuable than designing one. This is a methodological category error: MOFA+ is an unsupervised factor model for single-cell multi-modal data and SNF is a graph-fusion algorithm for bulk assays; they do not address the same problem as a workflow that wraps them. Replace the dismissive "single integration algorithm" framing with explicit acknowledgement that EMP, MOFA+ and SNF answer different questions at different layers of the stack.

8. **Unfair comparison to PALM and timeOmics on "algorithmic coverage."** The manuscript states that "Compared with PALM [R39]… EMP offers broader algorithmic coverage but less depth in causal temporal modelling." Given the same paper's later admission that EMP lacks time-aware wrappers, the "broader algorithmic coverage" half of the comparison is not supported. Either qualify the comparison to algorithmic breadth in the modules that EMP actually advertises, or acknowledge that PALM and timeOmics provide longitudinal capability EMP does not.

9. **Promotional language about EMP that exceeds R1's evidence.** Multiple formulations read as advocacy rather than balanced assessment: "lower the cost of going from raw reads to a host-microbiome model in one environment," "EMP is the most coherent but not yet a complete solution," "principled, microbiome-aware orchestrator," "places it on the more durable side of that divide," and the closing "its long-term value will depend on how seriously the community takes … in the versions that follow." Strip the promotional register; report what EMP demonstrably does per R1 and what limitations the manuscript itself enumerates.

10. **Editorial interpretation attributed to specific studies.** The phrase "Sharma and colleagues show that even when MOFA+ identifies multi-omics clusters with distinct long-term prognoses in breast cancer, these do not align cleanly with clinically deployed intrinsic subtypes [R25]" presents an interpretive claim as a finding. The R25 card reports identification of three clusters with long-term prognostic divergence validated in METABRIC and TCGA; it does not state that alignment with intrinsic subtypes is poor. Either rephrase as "Sharma et al. did not evaluate alignment with clinically deployed intrinsic subtypes" or cite full text.

11. **Missing key tools for a comprehensive review.** The review omits several widely used tools that any reviewer in this area would expect to see discussed, even briefly: Seurat (single-cell integration; R26 names it a top performer but no tool description is given); Anvi'o (the dominant multi-omics platform for microbial communities with explicit 'omics visualization and integration); MaAsLin2 and ANCOM-BC (microbiome differential-abundance standards increasingly used inside integration pipelines); mixMC (the mixOmics microbiome variant); O2PLS / JIVE / MCIA / RGCCA (canonical multi-block integration methods, none of which appears in the manuscript); and PaintOmics / 3Omics (pathway-level multi-omics web tools in the same niche as the Analyst suite). At minimum, one paragraph justifying their omission is required.

12. **Evaluation criteria are uneven across the dimensions a reviewer should expect.** Reproducibility, data structures and to some extent scalability are discussed, but documentation, maintenance, community uptake and active development are addressed only in passing. For a manuscript that bills itself as treating "software as the primary object of comparison," a systematic table evaluating each tool against the same checklist (scalability, data structure, interoperability, documentation, maintenance status, reproducibility infrastructure) is required and currently absent.

13. **MicrobiomeAnalyst "four browser-accessible modules" claim is not in R9.** R9's card describes the tool's coverage of 16S marker gene and shotgun metagenomic/metatranscriptomic data but does not specify the number of modules. Replace with "browser-accessible modules" or cite R9 directly.

14. **PathIntegrate cited as a "knowledge-driven" method in two forms.** In the taxonomy section the manuscript uses [R29] for "PathIntegrate… scores samples against pathway databases prior to multi-view modelling"; in the methods section it uses [R30] for "PathIntegrate transforms multi-omics data to single-sample pathway scores before applying single- or multi-view predictive models." These describe the same tool; pick one citation and use it consistently.

## Minor comments

1. "It is sharpened again in clinical cohorts" (Introduction) is grammatically clumsy; rephrase as "The concern is sharpened further in clinical cohorts."

2. The introduction contains a sentence that begins "A second consequence is that software itself has become a first-class research output," but the section it introduces is really the second consequence of integration as a discipline, not of the previous paragraph's argument. Add a bridging clause.

3. "The earliest generation of microbiome software did not attempt integration at all" is asserted without citation; the surrounding section relies on [R6, R8, R9], none of which makes this historical claim. Soften to "Many widely used microbiome tools predate the explicit multi-omics era."

4. "marker-gene" should be defined once on first use (e.g., "16S rRNA marker-gene amplicon data").

5. "Snakemake" vs "Nextflow" capitalization is consistent in the manuscript, but neither pipeline manager is introduced or referenced at first use; a one-sentence gloss would help non-specialist readers.

6. The phrase "the wave of newer deep matrix factorisations and GNN frameworks" conflates methods of different lineages (matrix factorisation vs. graph learning); specify what is meant.

7. "TMODINET couples self-attention with adaptive graph dynamic learning and Dirichlet/Dempster-Shafer uncertainty estimation for trustworthy cancer diagnosis" — R24 supports this, but "trustworthy" is the authors' own framing and reads as endorsement; rephrase to "for cancer diagnosis with explicit uncertainty quantification."

8. The single-cell benchmark paragraph says algorithms "should not be assumed to transfer" outside the benchmark conditions — correct but could be tightened by listing the specific conditions that fail (cell-type heterogeneity, batch-effect structure, ground-truth provenance).

9. In Section "Critical perspectives and controversies," the sentence on Borisov et al. quotes R21 directly; that is the only direct quotation and it is properly attributed. However, the manuscript also attributes specific phrasing to Hu et al. (comment 6 above) without quotation marks; remove the quotation marks or align with the card's wording.

10. "the user's daily commands can be composed using a natural-language-style syntax" — see Major comment 1; this is the same unsupported claim and must be removed.

11. The CRC benchmarking paragraph claims that "the most rigorous integrative-clustering benchmarks to date remain those anchored to TCGA" without comparator. The phrasing is an editorial superlative; if it is to remain, cite the comparative evaluation.

12. "Several limitations remain under-characterised in the existing literature" (recommendations section) — this paragraph lists limitations of other tools without citing the specific papers that fail to address them; add citations for each limitation claimed.

13. "the cumulative evidence nevertheless indicates that mature ecosystems (Bioconductor, QIIME 2, Snakemake/Nextflow) produce more reusable artefacts than heterogeneous ad-hoc pipelines" — "cumulative evidence" is unsupported; either cite the specific comparisons or replace with "we argue that …".

14. MicrobiomeAnalyst is referred to both as supporting "16S, metagenomic and metatranscriptomic profiles as essentially single-omics inputs" (Section "Microbiome-centred multi-omics tools") and as a platform "consolidated what had previously required stitching together QIIME 2… phyloseq… and bespoke R scripts" (Section "Web servers"). Both are consistent, but the metatranscriptomic capability should be flagged as the partial exception the text itself notes.

15. The phrase "the tools reviewed above" is used several times to refer to different subsets; specify the subset each time.
```

# Reviewer 2 — microbiome & host–microbe biology (end user)

## Summary assessment

The manuscript sets out to compare multi-omics integration software with EMP as its focal case, but the EMP-centric narrative is undermined by several unsupported claims, promotional framing, and asymmetric standards of evidence. Card R1 describes EMP as a five-module Bioconductor-based microbiome multi-omics workflow but does **not** substantiate recurring claims about a "natural-language-style interface", specific component dependencies (mixOmics, timeOmics, netOmics, metaQuantome), explicit longitudinal support, or the precise modality list the manuscript assigns to it. The critical lens applied to general tools is conspicuously softer when the same lens is turned on EMP. **Major revision required** before the manuscript can be considered for publication; the EMP section in particular must be rewritten so that every empirical claim is traceable to R1 or removed.

## Major comments

1. **"Natural-language-style interface" — not supported by R1.** The manuscript states three times that EMP is "invoked through a natural-language-style interface" (Introduction; "EasyMultiProfiler (EMP): design…" section, twice). Card R1 makes no mention of a natural-language, command-style or chat-style interface — only that EMP exposes a five-module pipeline built on SummarizedExperiment/MultiAssayExperiment. **Fix:** delete all three occurrences or replace with a specific, R1-supported description of how the user interacts with EMP (e.g., "R functions dispatching the five modules"). If the natural-language claim is in the original paper but outside the card excerpt, qualify it explicitly and cite the section.

2. **Listing of specific EMP components is unsupported.** The paragraph "drawing on — rather than re-implementing — components such as mixOmics-based PLS/DIABLO integration [R3, R44], timeOmics-style longitudinal modelling [R36], netOmics-style network interpretation [R37] and metaQuantome-style metaproteomic and metatranscriptomic quantitative analysis [R42]" attributes to EMP four specific algorithmic dependencies that R1 does not document. Card R1 lists the modules but does not name these engines. **Fix:** either cite the specific section of Liu et al. that establishes each dependency, or replace the sentence with what R1 actually says (e.g., "EMP exposes interfaces to upstream taxonomic profilers and downstream statistical routines; the specific engines it wraps in each module are not enumerated here").

3. **Unsupported modality list for EMP.** "EMP is microbiome-centric rather than universal, designed to handle metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics and metabolomics alongside host expression [R1]" — R1's excerpt names microbiome/host-microbiome multi-omics in general but does not enumerate these five modalities. Given that the manuscript elsewhere concedes EMP is "principally a workflow and integration layer, not an integration method in itself," the precise modality list should be sourced to Liu et al. or softened. **Fix:** "designed for microbiome and host-microbiome multi-omics studies, with documented support for [list sourced to R1]".

4. **Longitudinal-support claim for EMP is unsupported and contradicted by the manuscript's own later text.** "The longitudinal support emphasised in EMP's design also overlaps with what timeOmics [R36] and netOmics [R37] offer" and "time-series modelling" being listed among EMP's modules (line in §Microbiome-centred tools) are not in R1. The same manuscript later concedes that "EMP … does not currently advertise time-aware wrappers comparable to timeOmics; users with longitudinal host–microbe cohorts must therefore compose EMP with an external temporal module". **Fix:** remove the longitudinal-support claim from the architecture and EMP-design sections, and let the existing critique stand on its own. Do not let §Microbiome-centred tools contradict the §Data structures section.

5. **Promotional / asymmetric tone for EMP.** Phrases such as "EMP is the most coherent but not yet a complete solution" (Microbiome-centred tools section), "a principled, microbiome-aware orchestrator" (closing paragraph), and "the natural-language-style interface … is its novelty" (EMP design section) are value judgements, not evidence. Worse, the manuscript applies a far harsher standard to competing tools (e.g., MOFA+, MOGOLA, BioNeuralNet are each flagged for "TCGA-tuned hyperparameters", "interpretability is uneven", "self-reported comparisons", "dataset-dependent variability"), while EMP is described in qualitative terms with no comparable audit. **Fix:** apply the same critical standard to EMP. State explicitly what benchmarks EMP does and does not report, what evidence supports each module, and where the architecture has known failure modes. If no independent benchmark exists, say so and stop calling it "the most coherent".

6. **Unfair / unsupported comparisons to alternatives.** Three specific comparisons are unfavourable to the named alternatives and not supported by R1:
   - "Compared with phyloseq … EMP explicitly targets the host-microbiome interface" — phyloseq's R6 card does not say it lacks host-omics; it is routinely combined with host-side Bioconductor objects in practice.
   - "Compared with QIIME 2 … EMP is lighter on raw data processing and heavier on cross-omics synthesis" — neither claim is sourced to R1 or R8.
   - "Compared with PALM … EMP offers broader algorithmic coverage but less depth in causal temporal modelling" — the "broader algorithmic coverage" claim has no R1 basis.
   **Fix:** remove the "broader coverage" assertion; for the phyloseq/QIIME 2 cases, restrict claims to what is in R1 and R6/R8 respectively (e.g., "EMP is positioned as a wrapper around community profilers rather than as a replacement for QIIME 2's raw-data processing").

7. **Unsubstantiated "demonstration cases" claim.** "The demonstration cases illustrate that the workflow can be run, not that it outperforms alternatives on quantitative benchmarks such as subtype recovery, integration error or computational cost [R1]." R1's card excerpt does not enumerate demonstration cases. **Fix:** either source the demonstration cases to a specific section of Liu et al., or replace with the neutral statement "The paper does not report head-to-head benchmarks against mixOmics, timeOmics or netOmics on subtype recovery, integration error or computational cost".

8. **Incorrect "scAI" attribution to R26.** "Seurat, MOJITOO and scAI/UINMF-family methods for integration as the most consistent performers [R26]" — R26 names "totalVI, scArches, LS_Lab, Seurat, MOJITOO and UINMF". "scAI" does not appear. **Fix:** replace "scAI/UINMF-family" with "UINMF-family" or remove "scAI".

9. **Self-contradictory evaluation framework.** The manuscript rightly criticises the field for benchmarking on TCGA alone (§Benchmarking evidence; §Critical perspectives), yet the proposed yardstick for EMP — "does it outperform mixOmics, timeOmics or netOmics?" — implicitly demands exactly the kind of TCGA-style head-to-head the review argues against. If the review's position is that no tool should be judged by such benchmarks, it cannot use that yardstick to demote EMP. **Fix:** either state and defend a different evaluation criterion for EMP (e.g., reproducibility metrics, ease of use, ecological validity in microbiome cohorts) and apply it to the other tools too, or acknowledge the inconsistency.

10. **MicrobiomeAnalyst consolidation claim is unsupported.** "MicrobiomeAnalyst [R9] consolidated what had previously required stitching together QIIME 2 [R8] for upstream processing, phyloseq [R6] for taxonomic object handling and bespoke R scripts for differential abundance, into four browser-accessible modules" — R9's abstract does not make this historical claim; the phrasing presents author synthesis as established fact. **Fix:** rephrase as "In practice, a 16S/metagenomic analysis has often required combining QIIME 2 for upstream processing, phyloseq for object handling, and bespoke R scripts for differential abundance; MicrobiomeAnalyst offers a browser-based consolidation of these steps".

11. **Mischaracterisation of phyloseq's scope.** "phyloseq … remains the de facto low-level data layer on which many downstream analyses are built [R6]" and "Compared with phyloseq [R6], which excels at object-oriented single-omics census analysis but does not natively integrate host-omics layers". R6's abstract supports the first claim but not the second. **Fix:** remove "does not natively integrate host-omics layers" or qualify with a citation from outside the provided cards.

12. **EMP treated as both "convenience layer" and "deliberate advance".** The manuscript oscillates between describing EMP as "a curated wrapper" / "convenience and reproducibility layer" and crediting it with substantive design choices (MultiAssayExperiment anchoring, five-module decomposition, chained pipeline). Without R1 evidence for the latter, the manuscript cannot have it both ways. **Fix:** decide on one framing and stick to it; if EMP is principally a wrapper, the five-module decomposition should be presented as Liu et al.'s architectural contribution with citation to the specific section of R1.

## Minor comments

1. The R21 quote is paraphrased twice in the manuscript; both occurrences should carry the same wording, or be presented as paraphrases with quotation marks only where exact.
2. The R50 description ("with validation on simulated data and glioblastoma samples") should be confirmed against R50's wording ("simulated data and real data protocols"); the manuscript uses the gloss in several places.
3. R12's "F-score improvements of 7-24 %" appears in the manuscript at three locations (§General-purpose, §Recommendations, §Critical perspectives) without ever being challenged; given the review's stance that TCGA-only benchmarks are suspect, at least one of these mentions should flag that the gain was measured only on six TCGA cohorts.
4. The 335-patient figure for the breast cancer MOFA+ application [R25] should specify "transcriptomic, proteomic and metabolomic" consistently with the R25 abstract; the manuscript does this correctly, but earlier (§Benchmarking) it omits "metabolomic" when describing the same study.
5. "End-to-end" is used for CAEncoder (R48) and MCluster-VAEs (R38) with identical wording — fine, but the reader will benefit from a one-line reminder of what "end-to-end" denotes here (joint optimisation, not just pipeline).
6. "Snakemake pipeline" for gNOMO (R43) and "Nextflow" for the FAIR Digital Object workflow (R27) should be qualified — these are workflow managers, not algorithms; the manuscript mostly handles this correctly but slips occasionally.
7. "metagenomic, metatranscriptomic, metabolomic and host proteomic" (§Microbiome-centred tools, citing R55) is correct for R55 but the parallel list in the EMP design section does not match R1; cross-check the modality inventories between these two sections.
8. The discussion of "scAI" should be removed as noted in Major Comment 8; ensure no other occurrences remain.
9. The phrase "FAIR Digital Objects" is used in §Data structures and §Recommendations but should be defined once on first use (R27's framing is "Research Object Crate").
10. The MuSA description (R41) attributes "breast-cancer datasets" specifically — fine, but the manuscript uses MuSA as a general radiogenomic example in one sentence and a breast-cancer example in another; pick one or qualify.
11. The benchmark claim that "SNF gave the best subtype classification accuracy" (R17) is repeated three times; one occurrence should note that R17 itself reports this is highly dataset-dependent (which the manuscript does elsewhere — reconcile).
12. The statement that "the workflow does not replace specialised longitudinal or causal-inference frameworks such as PALM or timeOmics" is fair, but the manuscript should explicitly state that R1 does not claim longitudinal equivalence either, to avoid implying R1 overreaches.
13. The §Recommendations section recommends EMP "as one component of an ecosystem" — this is reasonable, but is contradicted by the §Microbiome-centred tools statement that EMP is "the most coherent". Choose one.
14. The intro's phrasing "contemporary cohort and single-cell studies increasingly require that molecular layers be modelled jointly" is a strong claim; the only card that supports a "require" framing at all is R55 (clinical IBD). Either downgrade to "increasingly benefit from" or add an explicit citation to a position paper.
15. The R10 / MUUMI description ("combining SNF with interactome inference to identify robust molecular signatures") is fine, but the manuscript should not present MUUMI as a general-purpose framework; R10's model is "idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, THP-1 macrophages", and the review should acknowledge this limited validation rather than citing MUUMI as a generic alternative.

# Reviewer 3 — editor, citation integrity & balance

## Summary assessment

This manuscript offers a broad, mostly accurate survey of multi-omics integration software and uses EasyMultiProfiler (EMP) as its focal case study. The taxonomic structure and overall argument are sensible, and the citation density is high. However, several claims about EMP (its "natural-language" interface, its compositionality/phylogenetic handling, its lack of time-aware wrappers) and a few claims about alternatives (e.g., EMP's reproducibility advantage over MicrobiomeAnalyst, the precise modality list of the single-cell analyst, the R17 claim about single-omics baselines) go beyond what the evidence cards support. Citation handling is inconsistent in two places (PathIntegrate, BioNeuralNet) and one factual slip occurs (MOFA vs MOFA+). **Recommendation: major revision required** before acceptance to (i) align EMP claims with the R1 card, (ii) fix citation inconsistencies, and (iii) soften or substantiate editorial inferences.

## Major comments

1. **Introduction, single-omics baseline claim — unsupported by R17.** The sentence "yet its margin over the best single-omics baseline was highly dataset-dependent" is attached to [R17]. The R17 card supports that "no single method dominated across all metrics" and that performance was sensitive to the simulated correlation structure, but it does *not* report a comparison between integrative methods and a best single-omics baseline. *Fix:* either remove the clause or rephrase as "performance was sensitive to the correlation structure imposed on the simulated data, with no method dominating across metrics", which is what R17 actually supports.

2. **EMP critique on compositionality and phylogenetic autocorrelation — not in R1.** In the Microbiome section the manuscript asserts: "EMP's analytical modules inherit the assumptions of their underlying algorithms, several of which — ordination, differential abundance, network construction — do not directly address compositionality or phylogenetic autocorrelation". The R1 card describes the five modules but is silent on compositionality or phylogenetics. *Fix:* either cite the EMP paper's own discussion of these gaps (which requires more than the card) or recast the sentence as the reviewer's assessment, e.g., "In the reviewers' reading of the available documentation, EMP…".

3. **EMP's "natural-language-style interface" — not in R1.** The EMP section states EMP offers "a natural-language-style interface so that the user does not have to manage incompatibilities between upstream taxonomic profilers and downstream statistical engines". The R1 card describes the five-module SummarizedExperiment/MultiAssayExperiment-based design but does not mention a natural-language interface. *Fix:* delete the qualifier or cite the full EMP paper for this specific feature claim.

4. **EMP longitudinal-wrappers gap — presented as fact but unsupported.** "EMP does not currently advertise time-aware wrappers comparable to timeOmics" reads as an objective description; R1 does not enumerate EMP's limitations, so this is reviewer inference. *Fix:* phrase as "the EMP documentation summarised in [R1] does not foreground a dedicated longitudinal module comparable to timeOmics", or move into a clearly labelled "Reviewer's assessment" subsection.

5. **Citation inconsistency for PathIntegrate.** PathIntegrate is cited as [R29] in the Taxonomy section and as [R30] in the General-purpose section. R29 is the bioRxiv preprint; R30 is the published PLoS Comput Biol version. *Fix:* cite [R30] (the published version) consistently throughout.

6. **Redundant dual citation of BioNeuralNet.** The manuscript uses [R11, R18] for BioNeuralNet. R11 is the published *Bioinformatics* version; R18 is the arXiv preprint describing essentially the same tool. *Fix:* cite [R11] only.

7. **MOFA vs MOFA+ inaccuracy for Sharma 2024.** The Benchmarking section writes "Application of Multi-Omics Factor Analysis to a 335-patient breast cancer cohort…". The R25 card specifies that the study used **MOFA+**, not MOFA. *Fix:* replace "Multi-Omics Factor Analysis" with "MOFA+" for accuracy.

8. **Single-cell analyst modality list not supported by R20.** The manuscript enumerates "scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq, immune profiling, copy-number variation, cytometry and flow cytometry". The R20 card states only "six single-cell omics modalities plus spatial transcriptomics" without enumeration, and also introduces "scAI" alongside UINMF (not in R20 card). *Fix:* describe as "six single-cell omics modalities (per [R20]) plus spatial transcriptomics" rather than asserting a specific list.

9. **Citation cluster [R55, R56, R59] overreach.** The Introduction claims these three sources together document the expansion into "bulk and single-cell transcriptomics, epigenomics, proteomics, metabolomics, metagenomics, metatranscriptomics and spatial modalities". Individually: R55 emphasises metagenomics/metatranscriptomics/virome/metabolomics/host proteomics/transcriptomics; R56 covers DNA sequencing, expression and methylation; R59 covers spatial multi-omics. The combined list is broader than any single card documents. *Fix:* rephrase to indicate that these reviews collectively illustrate the breadth, not that each lists every modality.

10. **Mild promotional tone about EMP's positioning.** The EMP section opens with "addresses the long-standing fragmentation of microbiome multi-omics analysis". The R1 card says only that EMP "provides a unified, reproducible multi-omics data integration framework"; "addresses long-standing fragmentation" is editorial. Similarly the closing paragraph calls EMP "a coherent R-based entry into host-microbiome integration that gains leverage from its MultiAssayExperiment foundation". *Fix:* tone down to "consolidates microbiome multi-omics steps within a unified Bioconductor framework" or similar, matching R1's vocabulary.

11. **Asymmetric comparison against MicrobiomeAnalyst.** "Compared with MicrobiomeAnalyst [R9], EMP trades point-and-click accessibility for programmability and reproducibility". The R9 card describes MicrobiomeAnalyst only as a "web-based tool… for statistical, visual and meta-analysis"; it does not state that MicrobiomeAnalyst lacks reproducibility features. *Fix:* rephrase as "Compared with MicrobiomeAnalyst [R9], EMP shifts the user from a web GUI to a programmatic R workflow, with the trade-off in accessibility that implies."

12. **Editor's inference about broader claims.** Statements such as "the field's binding constraint is reproducibility rather than novelty" and the closing claim that "the field's near-term progress will be driven less by yet another deep architecture than by reproducibility, interoperability and biological validation" are reviewer opinions unsupported by any specific evidence card. *Fix:* mark them clearly as the reviewer's position (e.g., "In our view…") rather than as field consensus.

## Minor comments

1. The phrase "five interconnected modules — extraction, preparation, support, analysis and visualisation" is repeated almost verbatim in three sections (Introduction, Data structures, EMP). Consider stating it once and referring back.
2. Long compound sentences in the Taxonomy section (e.g., the opening paragraph and the "Taxonomy, while pedagogically convenient" paragraph) could be broken up for readability.
3. "Treat-to-target" (T2T) management is used in the EMP section without prior definition; define on first use.
4. The Critical perspectives section runs to six numbered controversies and overlaps with material in the Benchmarking section (TCGA dominance, reproducibility, clinical actionability). Consider consolidating.
5. The phrase "biological fidelity" / "methodological generality" in the final controversy is the reviewer's construct, not a citation; consider citing a reference that frames this trade-off or use quotation marks.
6. "scAI/UINMF-family methods" in the Benchmarking section: the R26 card lists "UINMF" only, not "scAI". Drop "scAI" unless a separate source supports it.
7. Section transitions from "Microbiome-centred tools" to "Web servers" could be smoother; the link sentence ("The natural complement…") appears abruptly.
8. Some citations clusters could be trimmed: e.g., [R3, R44] in the EMP section, where one reference often suffices.
9. Passive voice and nominalisation (e.g., "is in principle be recovered", "are not always made visible to the user") could be tightened throughout.
10. The Recommendations section could benefit from a brief introductory sentence specifying that these are the reviewer's synthesis, since some recommendations (e.g., "Users should therefore prefer tools with version-pinned releases") go beyond what the cited cards directly endorse.
