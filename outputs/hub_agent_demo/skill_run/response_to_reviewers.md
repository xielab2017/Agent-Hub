# Response to Reviewers

We thank the three reviewers for their detailed and constructive critiques. Below we respond point-by-point to each major comment, describing the specific changes made in the revised manuscript. Minor comments are addressed at the end of each reviewer's section.

---

## Reviewer 1 — GDF15 signaling and receptor biology

### Major Comment 1 — Bariatric surgery attribution to [ref. 3]
**Comment:** *"drugs and behaviors that broadly improve metabolic health—including metformin, structured exercise, and bariatric surgery—robustly increase circulating GDF15 in both rodents and humans [ref. 3]."* [ref. 3] mentions only metformin and exercise.

**Change:** The phrase "bariatric surgery" has been removed from the triplet. The sentence now reads: "drugs and behaviors that broadly improve metabolic health—including metformin and structured exercise—robustly increase circulating GDF15 in both rodents and humans [ref. 3]."

### Major Comment 2 — Citation pairing error for cachexia/heart-failure evidence
**Comment:** The bracketed citation [ref. 16; ref. 15] misattributes the tumor-bearing result to [ref. 16] (which is the heart-failure/PPP1R15A model) and pairs two unrelated models.

**Change:** The citations have been split correctly. The sentence now reads: "Experimental models confirm that neutralizing GDF15 in tumor-bearing mice restores muscle mass and physical performance [ref. 15], and that blocking GDF15 in murine heart failure prevents cardiac cachexia and slows disease progression [ref. 16]."

### Major Comment 3 — Missing discussion of the β-adrenergic mechanism ([ref. 11]) in the receptor-biology section
**Comment:** The passage citing only [ref. 6] for "central initiation" omits [ref. 11], which is the central paper on how hindbrain GFRAL activation is transduced to peripheral metabolic tissues via sympathetic/β-adrenergic signaling.

**Change:** A dedicated paragraph has been added to the receptor-biology section explicitly summarizing [ref. 11]: GDF15 → hindbrain GFRAL → sympathetic outflow → β-adrenergic signaling in liver, white adipose tissue, and brown adipose tissue → insulin sensitization. The paragraph also notes that this mechanism explains the dissociation between anorexia and insulin sensitization.

### Major Comment 4 — The "no peripheral role" claim is contradicted by [ref. 11]
**Comment:** The statement "The data therefore do not yet establish a direct peripheral signaling role for GDF15 outside the hindbrain" is contradicted by [ref. 11], which reports that GDF15 suppressed glucagon "independently of the GFRAL receptor."

**Change:** The sentence has been revised to: "the *anorectic* effects of GDF15 are abolished by GFRAL deletion [ref. 6; ref. 7], but GFRAL-independent metabolic actions — including suppression of glucagon and direct effects on intestinal epithelial barrier function [ref. 14; ref. 11] — have been reported." This framing acknowledges GFRAL-independent actions while preserving the central role of GFRAL in anorexia.

### Major Comment 5 — [ref. 5] (Borner et al., nausea/emesis) entirely uncited
**Comment:** [ref. 5] is a foundational paper characterizing GDF15-induced emesis in *Suncus murinus*, nausea/malaise-associated behaviors (pica, conditioned taste aversion), and GDF15's contribution to chemotherapy-induced malaise. None of these concepts appear in the manuscript.

**Change:** A new subsection has been added on the qualitative nature of GDF15-driven anorexia, citing [ref. 5]. The text now states that GDF15-induced anorexia is mediated by nausea/malaise rather than homeostatic satiety, that GFRAL activation in the AP/NTS engages emetic neurocircuitry, and that this has direct implications for the therapeutic window of GFRAL agonists as well as for GDF15's contribution to chemotherapy-induced anorexia and hyperemesis gravidarum.

### Major Comment 6 — Insufficient discussion of species differences
**Comment:** Rodent and primate/human biology are treated as interchangeable. [ref. 7] is the only card with nonhuman primate (cynomolgus monkey) weight-loss data and explicitly confirms GFRAL expression in "mice and humans."

**Change:** A focused paragraph on species comparison has been added. It explicitly cites [ref. 7] for the NHP weight-loss data and for cross-species confirmation of GFRAL expression in mice *and* humans, notes that NHP data show qualitatively similar pharmacology, and clarifies that formal GFRAL-knockout data exist only in mice. Mouse-only claims ([ref. 6]) and mouse+human claims ([ref. 7]) are now clearly distinguished throughout.

### Major Comment 7 — Title's scope ("brainstem *and* hypothalamic circuits") is unmet
**Comment:** The manuscript discusses only AP/NTS and never addresses downstream hypothalamic relays (parabrachial nucleus → central amygdala; NTS → PVH/ARC).

**Change:** A new short subsection has been added describing downstream neural substrates of AP/NTS GFRAL neurons, including projections to the parabrachial nucleus, central amygdala, and hypothalamic nuclei (PVH, ARC, LHA). The absence of primary citations for these specific projection patterns is explicitly flagged as a limitation of the field.

### Major Comment 8 — Pro-protein maturation claim not directly supported by [ref. 4; ref. 7]
**Comment:** Neither card explicitly describes GDF15 pro-protein/propeptide cleavage; the claim is true in the broader TGF-β superfamily literature but not attributable to these two citations on the basis of the provided text.

**Change:** The bracketed citations [ref. 4; ref. 7] have been removed from this sentence, and the statement is now presented as general TGF-β superfamily context without specific citation to the card-based evidence. The phrasing has also been made slightly more cautious to acknowledge that the precise protease(s) responsible have not been directly demonstrated in the cited evidence.

### Minor comments addressed
1. **"Hybrid ligand"** — The phrase has been removed.
2. **"Effector of beneficial metabolic adaptation"** — Wording softened to "candidate biomarker of beneficial metabolic adaptation," reflecting the correlational nature of the [ref. 3; ref. 12] evidence.
3. **ref. 12 component ranking** — Revised to state that low HDL-cholesterol, abdominal obesity, and elevated glucose are the three strongest individual components, with blood pressure and triglycerides contributing more weakly.
4. **"Metabolic hormone" dismissal** — Revised in light of [ref. 11]; the central-sympathetic relay is now explicitly invoked as the mechanism by which a hindbrain-restricted ligand functions hormonally.
5. **"Almost exclusively"** — Replaced with "exclusively, within the resolution of current assays."
6. **GFRAL-KO magnitude** — Now made explicit that GFRAL deficiency worsens diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance in [ref. 7], consistent with abolition of the anorectic/weight-loss signal.
7. **RET downstream effectors** — A sentence has been added noting that downstream GFRAL-RET signaling engages canonical RET effectors (AKT, ERK, PLCγ, MAPK), and that the absence of detailed characterization in the cited cards is acknowledged as a limitation.
8. **Smad-mediated transcription claim** — Softened to "the dominant effector of GDF15's metabolic and anorectic actions does not appear to be SMAD-mediated."
9. **Limitations paragraph** — Expanded to flag the near-total absence of conditional/inducible GFRAL knockouts in adult animals, addressing the germline-compensation concern specifically.
10. **Conclusion's "may obscure" claim** — Revised to be consistent with [ref. 11]; the central-sympathetic relay is now cited as the mechanism reconciling hindbrain-restricted receptor expression with hormonal action.
11. **RET requirement in humans** — A sentence has been added raising this translatability question.
12. **AP outside the BBB** — The area postrema's status as a circumventricular organ with direct contact with circulating GDF15 is now explicitly mentioned.

## Reviewer 2 — Aging, metabolism, and clinical translation

### Major Comment 1 — Citation swap between ref. 16 and ref. 15
**Comment:** Heart-failure cachexia is attributed to the tumor-bearing study and vice versa.

**Change:** Identical to Reviewer 1 Major Comment 2. The sentence now reads: "…tumor-bearing mice restores muscle mass and physical performance [ref. 15], and that blocking GDF15 in murine heart failure prevents cardiac cachexia and slows disease progression [ref. 16]."

### Major Comment 2 — Unsupported bariatric surgery claim
**Comment:** [ref. 3] does not mention bariatric surgery.

**Change:** Identical to Reviewer 1 Major Comment 1. "Bariatric surgery" has been removed from the list.

### Major Comment 3 — Overstated breadth of clinical correlations, particularly in IBD
**Comment:** The "10- to 100-fold" rise and "consistently correlate with anorexia, lean mass loss, disease severity, and mortality" framing overstates the IBD evidence, where [ref. 14] correlates GDF15 with CRP and intestinal permeability markers only.

**Change:** The sentence has been restructured to attribute the "10- to 100-fold" rise specifically to advanced cancer [ref. 4], and to specify disease-specific correlates: cachexia/anorexia/mortality in cancer [ref. 13; ref. 4], inflammation and intestinal permeability in IBD [ref. 14], and lean mass/protein intake in heart failure [ref. 16].

### Major Comment 4 — Misattribution of "chronological age" and "frailty" to [an uncited source (card 12)]
**Comment:** [an uncited source (card 12)] is a candidate-biomarker review; its abstract does not specifically establish GDF15 rising with chronological age or frailty in longitudinal data.

**Change:** [an uncited source (card 12)] has been removed from this citation triplet. The sentence now relies on [ref. 3; ref. 4] for the stress/disease-related rise, and [an uncited source (card 12)] is now cited only in a separate, more appropriate sentence that frames GDF15 as one of several candidate frailty biomarkers.

### Major Comment 5 — Internal tension regarding non-GFRAL signaling
**Comment:** The biology section's claim that "the data do not yet establish a direct peripheral signaling role for GDF15 outside the hindbrain" is contradicted by [ref. 14] (intestinal epithelial barrier) and [ref. 11] (β-adrenergic insulin sensitization, GFRAL-independent glucagon suppression).

**Change:** The claim has been qualified. The biology section now explicitly acknowledges GFRAL-independent peripheral actions on intestinal barrier [ref. 14] and on glucagon/insulin sensitivity [ref. 11], while preserving the conclusion that direct GFRAL-mediated peripheral signaling remains unsupported. [ref. 11] is now cited in the Biology section rather than only in the Introduction.

### Major Comment 6 — Key nausea/emesis literature omitted [ref. 5]
**Comment:** The introduction and biology sections ignore the nausea/emetic neurocircuitry demonstrated by [ref. 5] as the proximate driver of GDF15-induced anorexia.

**Change:** Identical to Reviewer 1 Major Comment 5. A new subsection on nausea/emesis has been added, citing [ref. 5] explicitly and connecting it to the therapeutic-window implications for GFRAL agonism in obesity.

### Minor comments addressed
1. **"Independently" vs. "largely independent"** — Wording changed to "largely independent of leptin, ghrelin, and glucagon-like peptide-1 [ref. 3; ref. 4]."
2. **~25 kDa dimer** — [ref. 7] is now cited directly for this quantitative claim.
3. **β-adrenergic mechanism in introduction** — [ref. 11] is now cited explicitly when introducing the concept of GDF15-mediated insulin sensitization in obese rodents.
4. **GFRAL tissue-restriction list** — The organ-level list (liver, adipose, skeletal muscle) has been retained but is now presented as the standard negative-tissue panel from the primary papers, with [ref. 6; ref. 7] cited.
5. **ref. 12 component ranking** — Revised to state that low HDL-cholesterol (8.13%), waist circumference (6.74%), and elevated glucose (4.91%) are the three largest individual contributors, in that order.
6. **an uncited source (card 12) framing** — Now framed as a candidate-biomarker review listing GDF15 among 44 candidates, rather than as direct evidence for age-related elevation.
7. **Therapeutic-landscape nausea discussion** — The therapeutic-landscape section now explicitly discusses whether chronic GFRAL agonism in obesity will run into the same nausea/tolerability ceiling observed with GLP-1 analogs, citing [ref. 5].

## Reviewer 3 — Handling editor (methods and citation integrity)

### Major Comment 1 — Swapped citations for cancer vs. cardiac cachexia
**Change:** Identical to Reviewer 1 Major Comment 2 and Reviewer 2 Major Comment 1. The citation pair has been corrected: muscle restoration → [ref. 15]; cardiac cachexia → [ref. 16].

### Major Comment 2 — Mis-citation for "human genetic data" on GFRAL/obesity
**Comment:** "human genetic data supporting a causal role for GFRAL variants in obesity remain modest and sometimes inconsistent [ref. 14; ref. 11]" — neither [ref. 14] (IBD) nor [ref. 11] (β-adrenergic) addresses human GFRAL variants.

**Change:** The citation pair has been replaced. The sentence now cites [ref. 3] (which explicitly mentions "some human genetic studies support an interrelationship with human obesity") together with [ref. 6] and [ref. 7].

### Major Comment 3 — an uncited source (card 12) over-extended to GDF15-specific induction mechanisms
**Comment:** [an uncited source (card 12)] (Cardoso et al.) is a broad biomarker-candidacy survey, not a mechanistic catalog of GDF15 inducers.

**Change:** [an uncited source (card 12)] has been removed from the GDF15-inducer sentence. The sentence now relies on [ref. 3; ref. 4] for induction mechanisms (mitochondrial dysfunction, ER stress, inflammatory cytokines, oxidative injury, oncogenic transformation). [an uncited source (card 12)] is now cited only in a frailty/biomarker-panel sentence where it is appropriate.

### Major Comment 4 — Missing Conflict-of-Interest / Funding / Author Contributions statements
**Change:** A Conflict-of-Interest statement, Funding statement, and Author Contributions section have been added to the manuscript in accordance with ICMJE and standard review-publication policy.

### Major Comment 5 — ref. 11's GFRAL-independent glucagon effect not reconciled with central-circuit framing
**Comment:** ref. 11 reports GFRAL-independent glucagon suppression during clamp, contradicting the body's strong central-only framing.

**Change:** Identical to Reviewer 2 Major Comment 5. The biology section now explicitly carves out [ref. 11]'s peripheral/glucagon mechanism as a documented exception, and [ref. 11] is now cited in the Biology section in addition to the Introduction.

### Major Comment 6 — ref. 12 exclusion criteria not disclosed
**Comment:** ref. 12 restricted to participants "free of diabetes and cardiovascular disease"; this materially limits generalizability.

**Change:** The sentence now explicitly states the cohort restriction: "in community-dwelling older adults without established diabetes or cardiovascular disease [ref. 12]."

### Major Comment 7 — ref. 12 quantitative magnitude underrepresented
**Comment:** ref. 12 reports a fully-adjusted mean increase of 9.34% (95% CI 5.16–13.7%), with component contributions of 8.13% (low HDL), 6.74% (waist circumference), and 4.91% (elevated glucose). The manuscript paraphrases these as "modestly higher" without a number.

**Change:** The quantitative estimates have been added. The text now states: "circulating GDF15 concentrations were 9.34% higher (95% CI 5.16–13.7%) in metabolically syndromic older adults, with low HDL-cholesterol (8.13%), high waist circumference (6.74%), and elevated fasting glucose (4.91%) as the largest individual contributors [ref. 12]."
