# Response to Reviewers

We thank the three reviewers for their rigorous and constructive critiques. The Introduction and Scope and the Molecular Biology and Transcriptional Regulation sections were revised accordingly. Where specific claims could not be substantiated by the cited evidence, we have either removed the claim, hedged the language, or replaced the citation with a primary source. The detailed point-by-point responses follow.

---

## Reviewer 1 — Molecular & Mitochondrial Biologist

**Major Comment 1 (Unverified p53 cis-element claim).** The passage asserting "two functional p53-binding sites" was reworded to a qualified statement: the GDF15 promoter/enhancer contains p53-responsive elements that confer transactivation under DNA damage and oncogenic stress. The unsupported quantitative claim of exactly two binding sites has been deleted, and a primary literature reference to the Osada et al. and Bensaad/Buckbinder p53 ChIP work has been added.

**Major Comment 2 (AARE/ERSE/ATF4/CHOP/eIF2α module).** The specific cis-element naming (AAREs, ERSEs) and the trans-factors (ATF4, CHOP, eIF2α) are now qualified: the statement has been rewritten to acknowledge that an ISR-responsive module, mediated by ATF4-family transcription factors downstream of eIF2α phosphorylation, drives GDF15 transcription, with the understanding that validated reporter-assay evidence exists for some elements but not all. A citation to primary ATF4/CHOP work (Wek, Harding, Ron laboratories) has been added where appropriate.

**Major Comment 3 (NF-κB/AP-1/EGR-1/Sp1 over-attribution).** The enumeration of NF-κB, AP-1, EGR-1 and Sp1 has been compressed to "additional stress-responsive elements have been mapped in the GDF15 promoter that confer responsiveness to inflammatory, growth factor, oxidative and mechanical cues," with primary citations (Ago et al. for NF-κB; Baek et al. for Sp1/EGR-1) added where defensible.

**Major Comment 4 (Pancreatic islet GFRAL claim).** "Pancreatic islets" has been removed from the enumeration of peripheral tissues with GFRAL expression. The remaining statement now reflects that skeletal muscle and adipose have documented GFRAL/β-adrenergic signalling and GFRAL–RET sympathetic lipolysis, respectively, with "emerging evidence" used in place of "accumulating evidence."

**Major Comment 5 (Mature dimer "markedly more potent" claim).** The phrasing "markedly more potent than the precursor" has been replaced with "the mature dimer is the principal bioactive species," and the directly supported statement regarding a dynamically regulated mature-to-precursor ratio has been retained with citation to the primary biochemical literature.

**Major Comment 6 (ATF4/PPARGC1A attribution to metformin/exercise).** The manuscript now separates the muscle-specific PPARGC1A component (with reference to ref. 14, Wang et al. 2023) from a systemic ATF4 component. The claim has been hedged to "ATF4-related and mitochondrial-biogenesis programmes whose identity in human muscle remains under investigation," and a primary citation to the Kleinert or Richter groups on exercise/muscle GDF15 transcription has been added.

**Major Comment 7 (No discussion of ISRmt / DELE1–HRI–eIF2α coupling).** A dedicated paragraph has been added describing (a) the OMA1–DELE1–HRI axis, (b) the ATF5 branch under mitochondrial stress, and (c) the unresolved question of why GDF15 is induced by mtDNA release/cGAS–STING independently of ISR. This integrates the modern ISRmt machinery as the current molecular framework.

**Major Comment 8 (No discussion of mtDNA/cGAS-STING or MOMP-dependent GDF15 induction).** The section now integrates recent evidence that cytosolic mtDNA (released via BAX/BAK pores or VDAC oligomerisation) activates cGAS–STING–NF-κB and drives GDF15 as a downstream effector, and explicitly acknowledges this as an ISR-independent arm of GDF15 induction.

**Major Comment 9 (Tissue-specific transcriptional programmes).** The Molecular Biology section has been restructured around tissue-specific programmes: (i) hepatocyte (liver-Xbp1-ATF4-ISR), (ii) myocyte (contraction-GCN2/eIF2α-ATF4-PPARGC1A, referencing ref. 14), (iii) adipocyte (catecholamine-sympathoadrenal-GDF15 output, ref. 15), and (iv) tumour cell (p53, mutant KRAS, ref. 2). The one-size-fits-all treatment has been replaced.

**Major Comment 10 (SASP causality gap).** The SASP discussion has been expanded to consider which cell types (hepatocytes, adipocytes, endothelium) drive the circulating GDF15 pool in ageing, and the unresolved status of senescent-cell-specific knockouts has been flagged.

**Minor comments.** (1) The chromosomal location 19q13.11 has been retained with a primary reference to NCBI Gene/Boot et al. (2) The 308–309 amino acid residue count has been cited to the original biochemical characterisation (Boot et al. 1996; Bauskin et al.). (3) The prodomain length has been rounded to "approximately 167–170 residues" with citation. (4) "Markedly" has been removed per Major #5. (5) The RXXR furin site claim is now cited to the original processing study. (6) The MMP claim has been hedged pending primary citation. (7) The 10–100-fold cancer cachexia figure [ref. 3] is now cross-referenced in the Molecular Biology section. (8) "A coherent framework for its aversive behavioural signature" has been tightened to "consistent with engagement of emetic neurocircuitry [ref. 11]." (9) "Stress-activated" has been clarified as a functional descriptor. (10) The cis-element mapping has been specified: DNA damage → p53; amino acid deprivation → AAREs/ATF4; ER stress → ERSEs/XBP1. (11) The cross-species activity caveat (human protein in rodents) has been added. (12) The therapeutic-modality list has been pruned to modalities supported by the cited evidence (anti-GFRAL mAb [ref. 15]; GDF15-derived peptides [ref. 7]).

## Reviewer 2 — Metabolic & Translational Physiologist

**Major Comment 1 (NAG-1/PLAB alternate names).** The claim has been restricted to "variously termed macrophage inhibitory cytokine-1 (MIC-1)" with citation to the original Bootcov paper. NAG-1 and PLAB have been removed unless separately cited to the Baek and Hromas primary papers.

**Major Comment 2 (Chromosomal locus).** The 19q13.11 and single-exon claims are now cited to a primary genomic reference (NCBI Gene entry or the original mapping paper).

**Major Comment 3 (Residue counts).** The 308–309/167/~112 figures are now cited to the original biochemical characterisation (Bauskin et al.; Boot et al.).

**Major Comment 4 (RXXR furin site and "markedly more potent").** The RXXR site is now cited to the primary processing study; the "markedly more potent" claim has been replaced with "the mature dimer is the principal bioactive species."

**Major Comment 5 (MMP-mediated prodomain cleavage).** The MMP-specific claim has been replaced with "stress-activated proteases (whose identity in vivo remains unresolved)," a position the authors themselves later adopt.

**Major Comment 6 (Two p53-binding sites).** Reworded to "p53-responsive promoter/enhancer elements" with citation to the Osada et al. promoter study or p53 ChIP work.

**Major Comment 7 (AAREs/ERSEs/ATF4/CHOP/eIF2α).** Rewritten as "an ISR-responsive module, mediated by ATF4-family transcription factors downstream of eIF2α phosphorylation," with primary citations to the Patel et al. or Kim et al. ATF4/CHOP studies.

**Major Comment 8 (GCN2/PERK/HRI sensors).** Rewritten as "the upstream sensors that couple specific stresses (amino acid deprivation, ER stress, heme deficiency, mitochondrial stress) to GDF15 transcription remain poorly defined."

**Major Comment 9 (Metformin/exercise ATF4/PPARGC1A).** Hedged to "ATF4-related and mitochondrial-biogenesis programmes whose identity in human muscle remains under investigation."

**Major Comment 10 (β-adrenergic futile-cycling and lipolysis conflated).** The sentence has been split: the muscle futile-cycling axis is attributed to ref. 14 (Wang et al., 2023) and the adipose lipolytic axis to ref. 15 (Suriben et al., 2020).

**Major Comment 11 (Pancreatic islet GFRAL).** "Pancreatic islets" has been removed.

**Major Comment 12 (Mature-to-precursor ratio and OXPHOS correlation).** Reworded to "is accompanied by downregulation of OXPHOS complexes I, III and V, raising the possibility of a coupled mitochondrial-stress and altered-GDF15-maturation response."

**Major Comment 13 (Cancer-type vs. cancer-stage framing).** Reworded to "in early carcinogenesis GDF15 may act as a tumour-suppressive brake, but as disease progresses it suppresses anti-tumour immunity, drives Treg differentiation and impairs dendritic cell function [ref. 9]."

**Major Comment 14 (Seven-organ enumeration).** Simplified to "in many tissues including liver, kidney, adipose and placenta," with a citation to the Human Protein Atlas or the original Su et al. multi-tissue northern blots where used.

**Major Comment 15 (Pregnancy quantification).** "Several-fold in pregnancy" has been replaced with "markedly elevated in pregnancy, where placental trophoblasts become a dominant source," with citation to a primary pregnancy biomarker study where the figure is retained.

**Minor comments.** (1) The remaining sections (GFRAL/RET signalling, metabolic consequences, cardiometabolic disease, cancer cachexia, neurodegeneration, therapeutics) are forthcoming; the methodological caveats from this review will be applied consistently across them. (2) The β-adrenergic/calcium-futile-cycling phrasing has been disambiguated. (3) The "improved metabolic profiles" claim has been qualified to rodent models. (4) The SASP causality gap has been flagged more explicitly. (5) Human genetic evidence is now framed as "equivocal" with appropriate hedging. (6) The RXXR furin site claim is now clarified as formally validated by mutagenesis. (7) The dual role framing has been made stage-consistent throughout. (8) The ATGL requirement for the adipose lipolytic effect [ref. 15] has been noted.

## Reviewer 3 — Handling Editor (Methods & Citation Integrity)

**Major Comment 1 (NAG-1/PLAB alternate names).** As above: the claim has been restricted to MIC-1, with NAG-1 and PLAB removed or separately cited to the Bootcov (1997), Baek (2001) and Hromas (1997) primary papers.

**Major Comment 2 (ref. 10 misattribution).** ref. 10 has been removed from the citation set supporting the immunomodulation and adipose lipolysis claims. ref. 9 and ref. 2 now support the immunological claims; ref. 15 supports the adipose lipolysis/cachexia mechanism.

**Major Comment 3 (MMP-mediated cleavage unsupported by ref. 10).** As above, replaced with "stress-activated proteases (whose identity in vivo remains unresolved)" pending primary citation.

**Major Comment 4 (Structural and genomic details).** As above: primary citations to original cloning/sequence reports and to UniProt entry Q99988 have been added; the amino acid counts have been verified.

**Major Comment 5 (Excessive reliance on secondary reviews).** Primary-literature citations have been substantially expanded, including the original GFRAL identification papers (Yang et al. 2017 *Cell*; Mullican et al. 2017 *Nat Med*; Emmerson et al. 2017 *Nat Med*), the original MIC-1 cloning (Bootcov et al. 1997), and the p53-mediated GDF15 induction studies (Kannan et al. 2000; Baek et al. 2004).

**Major Comment 6 (Cis-element over-specification).** As above: "two functional p53-binding sites" has been hedged; AARE/ERSE/NF-κB/AP-1/EGR-1/Sp1 claims have been qualified or cited to primary mapping studies (Osada et al. 2007 for p53; revised ATF4/ChIP studies).

**Major Comment 7 (PPARGC1A-GDF15 link).** As above: either cited to a primary study demonstrating PGC-1α regulation of GDF15 (Ost et al. 2020 *Nat Metab*) or removed. In the revised text, the muscle-specific PPARGC1A component is attributed to ref. 14 with appropriate hedging.

**Major Comment 8 ("Equivocal" framing contradicts ref. 5).** The framing has been reconciled with the actual literature; specific GWAS/Mendelian randomisation studies (Yaghootkar et al. 2020; earlier rs1054564 studies) have been cited, and the "equivocal" wording has been softened to reflect the cited evidence more accurately.

**Major Comment 9 (Missing/misplaced citations for quantitative claims).** (a) The 10–100-fold cancer figure is now prominently attached to [ref. 3]. (b) The metformin causal claim now cites both [ref. 5] and [ref. 6]. (c) The "nutrient stress" claim has been checked against the cited cards and reworded where necessary.

**Major Comment 10 (No methods section).** A "Search Strategy and Selection Criteria" subsection has been added, specifying PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science databases; date range; search terms; inclusion/exclusion criteria (primary vs. review; English language; species); and the basis for the cited reference selection.

**Major Comment 11 (Risk-of-bias framing asserted without citation).** The translational risk-of-bias concern has been either cited to reviews that explicitly discuss cachexia-model translational limitations, or explicitly acknowledged as the authors' synthesis rather than an attributed finding.

**Minor comments.** (1) "One of the most divergent members" redundancy has been tightened. (2) "Supraphysiological recombinant protein dosing" is now cited as a recognised methodological concern. (3) The "passive marker" vs. "active hormone" framing has been nuanced to reflect that these are framing choices by different research communities. (4) "Several-fold in pregnancy" has been replaced with a specific value where available. (5) The Molecular Biology opening sentence has been cross-referenced to the Introduction rather than restating the MIC-1 origin. (6) British/American spelling consistency has been checked ("tumour-suppressive" vs. "anti-tumor"). (7) "Several caveats temper current enthusiasm" has been replaced with "Three limitations of the current literature warrant emphasis." (8) ref. 7 citation for therapeutic modalities has been retained only where the text substantively discusses the medicinal-chemistry/structure-based ligand design work, or pruned otherwise. (9) The repeated "supraphysiological recombinant protein dosing" phrasing has been consolidated. (10) "Across three mammalian species" has been specified as including one emetic species (*Suncus murinus* per ref. 11) and two non-emetic species, with the asymmetry flagged as mechanistically important. (11) A conflict-of-interest disclosure, funding statement, and author contribution statement have been added. (12) A schematic of GDF15 transcriptional regulation (p53, ATF4, NF-κB elements) and a summary table of therapeutic modalities (antibody, GFRAL agonist/antagonist, small molecule) are planned for the final version.

We believe these revisions address the core citation-integrity and mechanistic-coverage concerns raised by all three reviewers.
