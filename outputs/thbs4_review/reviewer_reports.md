# Reviewer 1 — muscle & myokine biology

## Summary assessment

This manuscript provides a comprehensive and commendably cautious review of THBS4 as a candidate myokine, correctly resisting premature elevation to "established myokine" status while systematically mapping the existing mechanistic and circumstantial evidence. The treatment of cell-of-origin ambiguity, the intracellular-versus-extracellular activity dichotomy, and the conditional nature of THBS4's protective effects in dystrophin-glycoprotein-complex versus non-DGC backgrounds are particular strengths. However, several specific mechanistic claims overreach the cited evidence (notably a CD47 candidacy unsupported by THBS4 biology or by the cited cards) and one passage mischaracterises what R45 actually demonstrated, then uses the mischaracterisation to critique the field. **Recommendation: major revision** before the claims and citations are sufficiently well-grounded for a definitive review.

## Major comments

1. **Section 9 (par. 2): receptor candidacy — CD47 is an over-claim.** The passage "Candidate-based approaches should prioritize integrins, gabapentin receptor α2δ-1 and CD47, all implicated in THBS4 biology in endothelial and mesenchymal contexts [R30, R36]" is unsupported both by the cited evidence and by thrombospondin-family biology. CD47 is bound by thrombospondins through their type-1 repeats, which subgroup-B THBS4 specifically lacks [R45]. Neither R30 (P387 variant and macrophage adhesion) nor R36 (α2/α2δ-1-driven angiogenesis) mentions CD47 as a THBS4 receptor. **Fix**: delete CD47 from the candidate list, or replace it with a receptor that the cited work (e.g., integrin α2 or α2δ-1 in R36) actually supports, and acknowledge explicitly that an unambiguously validated receptor has not been identified.

2. **Section 9 (par. 3): R45 mischaracterisation.** The sentence "the original characterization of THBS4 as a highly muscle-enriched transcript essential for normal skeletal development [R45] is contradicted by the absence of skeletal or bone-mass phenotypes in null mice [R37]" misrepresents the cited primary literature. R45 (Lawler 1993) characterised THBS4 as a gene highly expressed in heart and skeletal muscle with lower expression in brain, lung and pancreas; it did **not** claim THBS4 is "essential for normal skeletal development." The developmental/osteogenic claim comes from R52 (Tucker 1995, chick embryo). Critiquing the field for failing to validate a claim R45 never made weakens the manuscript's own argumentation. **Fix**: replace with "the early developmental focus on THBS4 in muscle and osteogenic contexts [R45, R52]" or drop the [R45] citation.

3. **Section 7 (par. 2): incorrect citation grouping for the AAA claim.** The sentence "THBS4 is induced in endothelial cells by TGF-β1 via SMAD3 and acts as a pro-angiogenic matricellular factor through integrin α2 and the gabapentin receptor α2δ-1, and THBS4 knockdown accelerates abdominal aortic aneurysm progression in ApoE-/- mice [R13, R24, R36]" cites R13 and R36 alongside R24 to support an AAA conclusion that only R24 establishes. R13 concerns TGF-β1-driven tumour angiogenesis; R36 concerns endothelial pro-angiogenic properties; neither reports AAA progression. **Fix**: cite only R24 (or R24 plus the primary AAA paper) for the knockdown/aneurysm claim, and split the sentence so that R13 supports only the SMAD3-induction claim and R36 supports only the integrin/α2δ-1 pro-angiogenic claim.

4. **Section 6 (par. on vascular inflammation): P387 mislabelled as a "pro-angiogenic variant."** The passage "The P387 pro-angiogenic variant enhances macrophage adhesion to atherosclerotic lesions more potently than A387 [R30]" conflates two distinct activities. R30 establishes P387 as a macrophage-adhesion/accumulation-promoting variant; it does not characterise P387 as more pro-angiogenic than A387. The general pro-angiogenic activity of THBS4 is reported in R36 but without variant comparison. **Fix**: "The P387 variant, which more actively supports macrophage adhesion and accumulation within atherosclerotic lesions than A387 [R30]..."

5. **Section 6 (par. on P387): unsupported lesion-size qualifier.** The clause "more actively supports macrophage adhesion and accumulation within atherosclerotic lesions than the common A387 variant, without altering overall lesion size in ApoE-/- knock-in mice [R30]" introduces a lesion-size claim ("without altering overall lesion size") that is not present in the R30 card excerpt and is not consistently emphasised across the cited reviews. **Fix**: either anchor the lesion-size claim to the specific result reported in R30 (e.g., "while ApoE-/- knock-in mice expressing P387 developed comparable total lesion area to A387 controls, [R30]") or remove the qualifier.

6. **Section 3 (last par.): the secretomics-criteria statement is too sweeping.** "the field has not yet applied standard secretomics criteria to THBS4 — namely demonstration of signal-peptide-driven transit through the canonical ER–Golgi route, stimulus-coupled vesicular or exosomal packaging, and quantitative release from healthy, non-permeabilised myofibres under defined contractile or hormonal inputs" implies that no such criteria have ever been applied to THBS4. In fact, R12 and R14 establish ER–Golgi transit and stimulus-coupled (ER-stress/mechanical-load) secretion of THBS4 from cardiomyocytes, and a cardiomyocyte-targeted secretion-defective mutant precisely dissects the intracellular versus extracellular pools. The genuine gap is in **skeletal myofibre**-specific application. **Fix**: rewrite to make the cell-type qualification explicit — "the field has not yet applied these criteria to THBS4 in skeletal myofibres in the way that has been done for cardiomyocytes [R12, R14]."

7. **Section 2 (par. on Brody dissection): over-extension of R12.** The sentence "overexpression of the secretion-defective mutant failed to protect and paradoxically worsened cardiomyopathy on an mdx background [R12]" combines two distinct manipulations that the R12 card excerpt does not jointly describe — Thbs4 deletion worsens cardiomyopathy in mdx mice, while the secretion-defective mutant dissection addresses intracellular UPR versus secreted activities in cardiomyocytes. Whether overexpression of the secretion-defective mutant *itself* was tested on an mdx background is not made clear by the card. **Fix**: separate these observations more carefully — describe the Brody dissection (WT vs secretion-defective on UPR and sarcolemma) and then state the R12 mdx result as a separate clause (e.g., "and on an mdx background, Thbs4 deletion worsens cardiomyopathy [R12]").

8. **Section 1 (definition of "bona fide" myokine criteria): tacit endorsement of a circulating-concentration threshold.** The opening criteria — "regulated secretion by muscle cells, detectable circulating concentrations that change with contractile activity, and demonstrable bioactivity at non-muscle target sites" — are presented as field consensus, yet the manuscript itself later documents that none of the canonical features most often attributed to myokines (e.g., receptor identification, dose-response pharmacology) are met for THBS4. The text later correctly notes the absence of receptor and conditional-knockout evidence, so the criteria list is internally consistent, but a brief disclaimer that some of these criteria are themselves contested (and that the field has lowered the bar over time) would tighten the framing. **Fix**: add a clause acknowledging that the listed criteria are not universally applied and that several currently accepted myokines pre-date some of these benchmarks.

## Minor comments

1. **Nomenclature inconsistency.** The manuscript uses "THBS4," "Thbs4," "TSP-4," and "Tsp4" in close proximity. Modern convention is **THBS4** for the human gene/protein and **Thbs4** for mouse. "TSP-4" should be used only when directly quoting the older tendon/equine literature (Hauser 1995 [R51], Södersten 2006 [R43]).

2. **Term "matricryptic factor"** is introduced in Section 1 without definition. Either cite its origin (e.g., its usage in the matricellular-protein literature) or replace with a less loaded descriptor.

3. **Inconsistent abbreviation of transforming growth factor β.** Standardise to "TGF-β" throughout (currently mixed with "TGFβ").

4. **Omission of R38 in the muscle–brain axis discussion.** R38 (Benner 2013) shows that endogenous SVZ-derived astrocyte Thbs4 modulates Notch signalling after cortical injury and that Thbs4 is a secreted homopentamer acting on neural precursors. Given that Section 5 builds a muscle–brain narrative on R4 alone, a brief reference to R38 would strengthen the biological plausibility and acknowledge an additional paracrine-neural precedent.

5. **Section 4 opening sentence.** "The clearest evidence for TGF-β1-driven THBS4 induction comes from vascular and dermal contexts rather than from skeletal muscle" — the qualifier "rather than from skeletal muscle" is correct and well-handled, but the following sentences sometimes blur whether any TGF-β1 induction has been demonstrated in myofibres (it has not). Consider a single explicit statement: "TGF-β1-driven THBS4 induction has not been formally demonstrated in skeletal myofibres."

6. **Section 5: the R5 "sedentary males" specification.** The passage "six weeks of high-intensity aerobic training in sedentary males [R5]" is more specific than the R5 card excerpt ("humans"). Either confirm the cohort matches this description or soften to "previously sedentary humans."

7. **Section 7 (third-paragraph synthesis).** "the matricellular protein integrates opposing inputs to maintain proteostasis [R14, R15, R16, R22, R48]" over-attributes the "opposing inputs" framing — the idea that tonic TGF-β suppression paradoxically de-represses THBS4 originates specifically from R22 and R48; R14, R15, R16 do not establish opposing inputs. Narrow the citation set to R22 and R48.

8. **Section 9 (receptor paragraph): CD36 absent.** Given that integrin α2 is the only biochemically defined THBS4 receptor in the cards (R36), mention of CD36 — a known THBS1/TSP-1 receptor with some published cross-reactivity — would broaden the candidate list more defensibly than CD47.

9. **Section 6: LPL/VLDL phenotype framing.** The claim that Thbs4 deletion "directly links THBS4 to oxidative muscle lipid handling" (Section 1) is valid for R1 but is intra-muscular rather than endocrine; this is acknowledged later (Section 6: "an intra-muscular phenotype that does not establish THBS4 as an insulin-sensitising or lipid-handling hormone in the classical myokine sense"). To avoid the reader inferring an endocrine reading at first mention, qualify the original sentence with "intra-muscular" in Section 1 as well.

10. **Figure/table recommendation.** With ~60 references and ten sections covering mechanism, exercise, ageing and inter-organ biology, a single summary table mapping each criterion of the myokine framework (regulated transcription, secretion evidence, circulating detection, receptor engagement, in vivo phenotype) to the supporting cards and the muscle-specific gaps would significantly improve the reader's ability to assess each claim.

# Reviewer 2 — ageing, metabolism & translation

## Summary assessment

This is a comprehensive, well-organized narrative review that systematically maps THBS4 biology across muscle, heart, vasculature, fibrosis and ageing. The author correctly resists anointing THBS4 as a "bona fide" myokine and is appropriately sceptical about extrapolating cardiac and matricellular data to myofibre endocrine biology. The mechanistic synthesis is generally accurate, and the proposed experimental roadmap is sensible. **Recommendation: Major revision.** The manuscript contains at least one factual misattribution (mouse data cited as human), several claims that overstate what the underlying studies actually demonstrate, and insufficient quantitative anchoring for the central biomarker claim. With tighter evidence hygiene and a more disciplined separation of mouse from human data, the paper would be publishable.

---

## Major comments

1. **Section 10 (Conclusions), paragraph beginning "In humans, circulating THBS4 has been reported to decline with age…"** — *Problem*: This sentence attributes a human decline in circulating THBS4 with age to [R4] (Gan et al., 2019), but R4 is explicitly a *mouse* study ("Mass spectrometry reveals thrombospondin-4 is enriched in serum from young but not old mice"). The same misattribution recurs in Section 9 ("the only human evidence supporting circulating THBS4 as a metabolic signal comes from indirect serological observations in older or dystrophic cohorts rather than from prospective, biopsy-anchored studies [R4, R6, R8]"), which again groups the mouse Gan study with the human de Jong biomarker study. *Fix*: Either remove [R4] from the human claim or explicitly note that the age-related decline in circulating THBS4 has only been demonstrated in mice to date. Re-cast the claim as "In mice, circulating THBS4 declines with age [R4]; whether the same holds in humans is unknown."

2. **Section 5, paragraph beginning "A second, mechanistically distinct line of evidence implicates circulating THBS4 in the systemic biology of ageing"** — *Problem*: The phrasing "implicates" overstates the evidentiary status. R4 is a single young-vs-old mouse serum proteomic screen with no compartment-resolved source attribution; the muscle origin of the THBS4 deficit in old mice is not demonstrated, the magnitude is not quantified, and no human replication exists. Calling it a "mechanistically distinct line of evidence" creates a false sense of independent confirmation. *Fix*: Soften to "suggests" and explicitly state that the muscle-derived origin of the serum THBS4 deficit in old animals has not been demonstrated, and that no equivalent human proteomic comparison stratified by age has been published.

3. **Section 6, paragraph beginning "Thbs4-null mice subjected to angiotensin II infusion develop exaggerated cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, perivascular fibrosis, aortic dissection and a high incidence of aneurysms, yet preserve endothelial-dependent relaxation in resistance arteries [R34, R32]"** — *Problem*: This is correctly supported by R34 (aneurysm/dissection phenotype with preserved endothelial function) and R32 (Ang II cardiac phenotype), but the manuscript then collapses this complexity in Section 7 by stating Thbs4-/- mice develop "aortic dissection and aneurysmal disease, even while paradoxically preserving resistance-artery endothelial function" without flagging that these two phenotypes are mechanistically distinct and arise in different vascular compartments. The review should make clear that this "compartment-specific" framing is the most defensible synthesis (which it does in Section 8) rather than treating both as parts of a single "cardioprotective" narrative. *Fix*: When first introducing the Ang II data in Section 6, flag explicitly that the cardiac and large-vessel phenotypes are dissociable.

4. **Section 5, paragraph beginning "In a sex-stratified analysis of community-dwelling individuals aged 75 years and over…"** — *Problem*: The manuscript states that females were "matched for age, body mass index and Fried frailty index" and that "the published analysis does not formally exclude residual confounding by these factors." The R6 abstract does not specify that participants were matched on BMI or Fried frailty index; these methodological details appear to be the author's interpretation of the full paper rather than what can be verified from the card. This needs to be checked against the source. *Fix*: Either verify these details against the de Jong 2025 full text and remove them if not present, or qualify the statement as the author's reading of the methods. More importantly, the manuscript should report effect size, p-value, sample size and AUC (if provided) for the THBS4–frailty association, since the central biomarker claim depends on this single study.

5. **Section 2, paragraph beginning "Within the ER lumen, THBS4 engages the chaperone BiP/GRP78 and the stress sensor ATF6α…"** — *Problem*: The manuscript consistently uses "matricellular" alongside "intracrine" without clearly distinguishing them. ATF6α/BiP engagement inside the ER lumen is intracrine (action within the producing cell); matrix deposition after secretion is matricellular/paracrine. The terms are not interchangeable. The manuscript also uses "matricrine" (Section 3, paragraph on the intracellular itinerary) which is a less standard term that could be confused with "matricellular." *Fix*: Define the three terms once, in Section 2, and use them consistently: "intracrine" for ER-resident action, "matricellular/autocrine/paracrine" for matrix-localised action, and reserve "endocrine" only for actions on distant target organs.

6. **Section 1, sentence "demonstrated higher THBS4 protein levels associated with the microvasculature of oxidative, slow-twitch fibre beds [R44, R1]"** — *Problem*: This anatomical claim is attributed to both R44 (Arber 1995) and R1 (Frolova 2014), but R1's abstract mentions tendon, soleus ECM, LPL and VLDL uptake without referring to microvascular localisation. The microvascular enrichment claim likely originates with R44 and/or the Frolova full text. *Fix*: Verify the source and either drop R1 from this specific claim or replace it with the primary microvascular-localisation reference.

7. **Section 4, paragraph beginning "The clearest evidence for TGF-β1-driven THBS4 induction…"** — *Problem*: The manuscript correctly notes that all the SMAD3/SMAD2 induction data derive from endothelial cells, dermal fibroblasts and pulmonary smooth muscle, and explicitly states that "whether the same SMAD3/SMAD2 circuitry operates in skeletal myofibres remains untested." However, the section heading still describes this as "TGF-β/SMAD, hypoxia/HIF-1α, ATF6 and integrin engagement" without flagging that the first three axes have essentially zero direct support in skeletal myofibres. *Fix*: Reframe the section subheadings or lead paragraphs to clarify that this is a mechanistic *extrapolation* from non-muscle cell types, with skeletal-muscle-confirmed mechanisms limited to the ATF6α-BiP-THBS4 axis (R2, R12, R14).

8. **Section 9, paragraph beginning "Several ostensibly foundational observations do not survive close scrutiny…"** — *Problem*: The sentence "the original characterization of THBS4 as a highly muscle-enriched transcript essential for normal skeletal development [R45] is contradicted by the absence of skeletal or bone-mass phenotypes in null mice [R37]" overstates R45. The Lawler 1993 paper identifies THBS4 as "highly expressed in heart and skeletal muscle" but does not characterise it as "essential for normal skeletal development." The contradiction with R37 (no bone phenotype in null mice) is therefore weaker than implied. *Fix*: Reword to something like "the high skeletal-muscle expression reported in the original identification [R45] coexists with a lack of overt skeletal or bone-mass phenotype in null mice [R37], suggesting that the developmental role, if any, is redundant or compensated."

9. **Section 5, paragraph beginning "The most coherent ageing-related phenotype of THBS4 in skeletal muscle…"** — *Problem*: This paragraph builds an extended narrative ("chronic mechanical and oxidative burden imposed on aged myofibres may gradually unmask a protective reserve normally buffered by THBS4") that is reasonable speculation but is presented in declarative form. The same paragraph acknowledges that "Whether the same conditional logic applies to physiological ageing… remains untested." *Fix*: Mark the speculative reasoning explicitly as such ("we speculate that…"); do not let the prose confidently assert what the data have not shown.

10. **Throughout, but particularly Section 6, paragraph on the "closest approximation to a direct metabolic-myokine claim"** — *Problem*: The manuscript repeatedly frames the LPL/VLDL phenotype of Thbs4-/- soleus (R1) as a "metabolic-myokine" claim, when it is in fact an intramuscular phenotype that says nothing about endocrine action. The distinction is correctly drawn in the paragraph, but elsewhere (e.g. the conclusion) the LPL/VLDL observation is implicitly lumped into the systemic-metabolism argument. *Fix*: Be consistent that the LPL/VLDL phenotype is an *intra-muscular* metabolic role, not evidence for endocrine metabolic action. If anything, it cuts *against* a circulating-hormone model by suggesting the relevant biology is local.

## Minor comments

1. **Section 2, "TurboID"** — The trademark should be capitalised as TurboID (not turboID) on first use.

2. **Section 2, "Brody programme"** — This colloquial reference to the Brody-lab body of work (R12, R14, R16) is fine for a narrative review but should be glossed ("the body of work from the Brody laboratory") on first use.

3. **Section 5** — The statement that circulating THBS4 has emerged as a "candidate biomarker of early frailty" is supported only by R6 (de Jong 2025). The phrase "early frailty" should be tied explicitly to the pre-frailty/frailty framework of that paper rather than presented as a general claim.

4. **Section 6** — "gabapentin receptor α2δ-1" is correctly described as "an established drug target of gabapentin and pregabalin." This is a useful translational hook; a brief note that no one has yet tested whether pregabalin/gabapentin modulates THBS4 signalling would strengthen the translational framing.

5. **Section 7, discussion of the A387P polymorphism** — The 2.5- to 3-fold odds ratio for MI (R50) is for a specific replication case-control study; the original association (Stenina 2004, R54) had a different magnitude and ascertainment scheme. The review would benefit from a one-sentence caveat about the replication-vs-discovery distinction.

6. **Section 8, "the Brody programme demonstrates…"** — Replace with a neutral reference (e.g. "Brody and colleagues showed [R12, R14, R16]…").

7. **Section 9, the experimental roadmap** — The proposal to use HSA-CreERT2 is sensible, but the manuscript should acknowledge that ACTA1-Cre and MCK-Cre are not tamoxifen-inducible and will therefore delete Thbs4 during development; the recommendation should explicitly prefer HSA-CreERT2 (or similar) for the inducible experiments and reserve the constitutive Cre lines for confirmation only.

8. **Section 9** — Several cited references (R9, R10, R23, R24, R55, R56, R57) carry 2026 publication dates. While this is not impossible, the author should ensure each is cited with a DOI or article number rather than relying solely on the journal/year, to allow verification.

9. **Section 4** — The statement that "Thbs1 instead activates a PERK-ATF4 axis that drives lethal autophagy in cardiac myocytes [R20]" is correct, but the contrast could be sharpened by noting that THBS1 has a CD47-binding domain that THBS4 lacks, which may explain the divergent signalling.

10. **Section 10** — "On balance, the evidence is sufficient to designate THBS4 as a candidate myokine…" is a reasonable summary, but the conclusion would benefit from a one-sentence statement of what minimal experiment would, in the author's view, tip the balance toward "established myokine" status (e.g. a single study combining muscle-specific inducible knockout with paired serum/tissue proteomics and a distal-tissue phenotype). This would make the conclusion more actionable.

11. **Section 1** — "the contemporary view of skeletal muscle as an endocrine organ rests on the demonstration that contractile fibres release a heterogeneous secretome…" should briefly cite a primary demonstration (e.g. Steenberg 2000 or Pedersen 2003 IL-6 work) alongside the review citations, to anchor the historical claim.

12. **Section 3** — The phrase "qualifies as a genuine myokine, defined here as a factor synthesised by the contractile muscle cell and released into the extracellular space in a regulated fashion to act on local or distant targets" is a useful working definition; the same definition should be recapitulated in Section 10 so the reader is not required to scroll back to find it.

13. **Section 6, on the discordance between P387 allele data and germline Thbs4 deletion** — The review notes the apparent contradiction but does not propose a resolution. A short mechanistic comment (e.g. that P387 may gain an integrin-binding function absent from A387, while germline deletion removes both) would strengthen the section.

14. **Section 9** — The reference to "the only human evidence supporting circulating THBS4 as a metabolic signal comes from indirect serological observations in older or dystrophic cohorts [R4, R6, R8]" again groups the mouse study (R4) with human studies. Replace with "[R6, R8]" and add "in humans; [R4] in mice."

15. **Throughout** — The manuscript oscillates between declaring THBS4 a "candidate myokine" (appropriate) and implying it functions as one in framing sentences. A consistent rule — e.g. always use "candidate myokine" or "putative myokine" when describing THBS4 in muscle — would tighten the argument.

# Reviewer 3 — editor, methods & citation integrity

## Summary assessment

This is a thorough, well-organised review that responsibly tempers the "myokine" framing of THBS4 with explicit caveats, contextual heterogeneity, and clear experimental priorities. The critical-perspectives section and the roadmap in §9 are particular strengths. **Recommendation: major revisions.** Several specific claims overreach the cited evidence cards (notably a microvascular/oxidative-fibre localisation attributed to R44/R1, CD47 cited as a THBS4 receptor supported by R30/R36, and the "original" ATF6 demonstration attributed to the commentary R16). One omission claim (R57) is not directly supported by its card. A condensed, more disciplined prose style would also help.

## Major comments

1. **§1, paragraph 2 — microvascular/oxidative-fibre localisation is not supported by the cited cards.**
   *"…demonstrated higher THBS4 protein levels associated with the microvasculature of oxidative, slow-twitch fibre beds [R44, R1]."* The R44 card documents interstitial-cell expression and NMJ accumulation in adult muscle; the R1 card describes tendon collagen, soleus ECM, LPL activity, VLDL uptake, soleus mass and grip. Neither card establishes protein enrichment at microvessels of oxidative fibre beds. The same unsupported claim recurs in §3 ("THBS4 protein is enriched around microvessels of red, oxidative fibres … [R1]") and §6 ("THBS4 protein in red oxidative muscle is enriched at the microvasculature [R1]"). **Fix:** drop the microvascular claim or attribute it to a source that actually shows it; the R1-supported statement should be restricted to ECM composition, LPL/VLDL handling, soleus mass and grip, with R44 retained for NMJ/interstitial localisation.

2. **§9 — CD47 is not supported by the cited cards.**
   *"Candidate-based approaches should prioritize integrins, gabapentin receptor α2δ-1 and CD47, all implicated in THBS4 biology in endothelial and mesenchymal contexts [R30, R36]."* Neither R30 nor R36 mentions CD47; the cards describe integrin α2 and α2δ-1 as the established THBS4 receptors in endothelial work. **Fix:** remove CD47 (or, if you want to preserve it as a speculative candidate, state it as hypothetical and drop the citation, since none of your available cards substantiate CD47 engagement by THBS4 specifically).

3. **§2 — "the original demonstration … was therefore paradigm-shifting … [R16]".**
   R16 (Doroudgar & Glembotski, *Circ Res* 2013) is a *commentary* on Lynch et al., *Cell* 2012; it is not the primary report. Attributing the original demonstration to R16 is a citation error that propagates through §7 and §8 wherever "R16" is paired with "demonstration/protects cardiomyocytes from hemodynamic stress". **Fix:** cite R14 (Brody 2016) and the underlying primary work (Lynch et al., *Cell* 2012) for the original finding; keep R16 as commentary if it adds value, but do not present it as the source of the demonstration.

4. **§9 and §10 — claim that R57 "omits THBS4 entirely" is not directly supported by its card.**
   R58, R59 and R60 cards explicitly state "without specific mention of THBS4". The R57 card lists irisin/FNDC5, BDNF, IL-6, IGF-1, cathepsin B and GDF-15 as mediators of muscle–brain cross-talk, but does not affirm or deny THBS4 omission. Asserting that R57 omits THBS4 is therefore an unsupported inference. **Fix:** qualify the claim (e.g., "R58, R59 and R60 do not discuss THBS4, and R57's catalogue of muscle–brain mediators likewise does not include it") or remove R57 from the omission list.

5. **§8 — "The Brody programme".**
   Phrases such as "The Brody programme demonstrates that transit through the secretory pathway is required for sarcolemmal stabilisation" are opaque for a generalist reader and overstate a single thread of related papers as a coordinated programme. **Fix:** replace with "Brody and colleagues [R12, R14] showed…".

6. **Internal consistency — multiple reviews cited for "omitting THBS4" should be checked against cards.**
   Section 10 cites R55 and R56 in the same breath as R57–R60 for the omission argument. R55's card describes a 2026 synthesis of myokines/exerkines and inter-organ crosstalk but does not affirm THBS4's absence; R56 is a breast-cancer-focused exercise-oncology review whose card makes no statement either way. **Fix:** restrict the omission argument to the reviews whose cards explicitly confirm it (R58, R59, R60), and explicitly note that R55/R56/R57 were not directly verified for omission.

## Minor comments

1. **§1, last paragraph — "matricryptic factor"** is introduced without definition. Add a one-clause gloss (e.g., "an ECM-anchored or intracellularly acting protein whose bioactive fragments can be released under stress").
2. **§2, paragraph 4 — "essential quality-control checkpoint"**. "Essentially" / "essential" repetition; pick one.
3. **§3, last paragraph — "stimulus-coupled vesicular or exosomal packaging"**. "Stimulus-coupled" is the same idea as "regulated"; consider "regulated stimulus-coupled vesicular or exosomal release" only if you mean both.
4. **§4 — paragraph on MLL4.** The card for R15 supports MLL4→H3K4me1→Thbs4 in cardiomyocytes under pressure overload; the manuscript's wording "cardiomyocyte-specific deletion of Mll4" is consistent with the model but the card abstract does not state the deletion itself is cardiomyocyte-specific — soften to "deletion of Mll4 in cardiomyocyte models" if you are not certain from the underlying methods.
5. **§5 — "sex-specific regulation of THBS4 transcription in skeletal muscle"** is asserted as a plausible explanation for the male-only frailty signal, but no card supports a sex difference at the transcript level; qualify as "hypothesised".
6. **§6 — "without altering overall lesion size in ApoE-/- knock-in mice" [R30].** The card describes ApoE-/- knock-in mice but the model details are minimal; the manuscript should hedge ("in an ApoE-/- knock-in model") rather than asserting lesion-size neutrality as established.
7. **§7 — "metoprolol halves post-infarction … TSP-4 expression"** [R49]. Card says "~50% decrease"; "halves" is consistent but consider "~50% (i.e., approximately halves)" for precision.
8. **§7 — "the same matricellular cues can have opposing net effects depending on the resident fibroblast phenotype".** A reasonable synthesis, but not directly supported by any single card; frame as the review's interpretation rather than as a documented finding.
9. **§8, point 3 — "RGD-domain usage"** is invoked to differentiate thrombospondins, but THBS4 lacks the type-1 repeats and the central RGD context of THBS1; either specify the relevant domain (e.g., T3R/TSP-C) or drop the RGD reference.
10. **§8, point 5 — "the field's accumulated data support a model in which THBS4 is broadly beneficial across striated muscle and the vessel wall".** The Section 7/§6 data on kidney fibrosis (R25), skin scarring (R29, R33), aneurysm (R34) and restenosis (R35) show harm in specific contexts — "broadly beneficial" is too generous. Replace with "broadly protective in striated muscle and the pressure-overloaded vessel wall, but context-dependent and potentially deleterious in fibrotic kidney, scarring skin and certain vascular-remodelling settings".
11. **§9 — the experimental roadmap item "TurboID fusions targeted to the ER–Golgi intermediate compartment"** is plausible but not in any card; flag as a methodological proposal.
12. **§10, third paragraph — "what the data do not yet show is that circulating THBS4 in aged or exercised humans is mechanistically required for any distal tissue response"** is the strongest, most defensible single sentence in the conclusions; consider promoting it to the abstract.
13. **Repetition.** The same sentence-level claims (intracellular vs. extracellular THBS4 dichotomy; conditional protection across DGC genotypes; need for conditional alleles) recur in §1, §3, §7, §8, §9 and §10. Consider one canonical statement per claim with cross-references elsewhere.
14. **Length.** §6 and §7 in particular duplicate material; a 10–15 % reduction would improve readability without losing coverage.
15. **Style — "… make a prima facie case for re-evaluating THBS4 …"** (§1), "… considered alongside reviews … make a prima facie case …" — fine, but the second use adds little; tighten.
16. **The card set contains [R38] (Benner et al., SVZ astrogenesis via THBS4/Notch), [R46] (Xu et al., vascular graft remodelling), and [R52] (Tucker et al., chick osteogenesis) that are not cited.** If the muscle–brain axis argument is to rest on young-vs-old serum [R4], R38 is directly relevant astrocytic/Notch biology and its omission weakens the §5 narrative.
