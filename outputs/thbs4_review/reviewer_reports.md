# Reviewer 1 — muscle & myokine biology

## Summary assessment

This is a comprehensive, well-structured review that is appropriately cautious about classifying THBS4 as a myokine — correctly identifying that the strongest evidence (Willis et al., R5) is transcriptomic/network inference rather than direct demonstration of regulated secretion, circulating pentameric protein, and receptor-mediated distal bioactivity. The mechanistic synthesis is largely accurate, the discussion of intracellular versus extracellular pools is well-handled, and the critical perspectives on context-dependence are a genuine strength. However, the Introduction over-claims on fibrosis directionality and the metabolic–contractile axis, several muscle-specific concepts (satellite cells, regeneration, training-modality effects) are missing, and a few anatomical details exceed what the cited abstracts can support. **Recommendation: Major revision** — the paper is a potentially valuable synthesis, but the over-claims in the Introduction and the omission of regenerative/myogenic-stem-cell biology should be corrected before publication.

## Major comments

1. **Section: Introduction, paragraph 1.** The passage "promoting rather than inhibiting angiogenesis, attenuating rather than driving fibrosis, and acting from both inside and outside the cell—diverges so sharply from that of other thrombospondins [R8]" presents fibrosis directionality as a settled property of THBS4. The evidence cards directly contradict this: [R23] shows THBS4 is *pro-fibrotic* in chronic kidney disease (UUO model, TGF-β1-driven), [R22] shows reduced THBS4 *inhibits* TGF-β1/Smad-driven trophoblast invasion in preeclampsia, and [R51] shows stromal mutant-p53-driven THBS4 secretion fuels HER2+ tumour progression via PI3K/AKT. The manuscript itself acknowledges this dichotomy later (Critical Perspectives), so the Introduction should be reworded to reflect that the anti-fibrotic effect is context-dependent (e.g., "attenuating fibrosis in pressure-overloaded myocardium while amplifying TGF-β-driven fibrosis in chronic kidney disease and tumour stroma").

2. **Section: Introduction, paragraph 3.** The passage "directly linking THBS4 to the metabolic–contractile axis most vulnerable in ageing and type 2 diabetes [R1]" over-extrapolates. Frolova et al. [R1] report *local* ECM-dependent effects (smaller soleus, reduced LPL activity and VLDL uptake within the same tissue that expresses THBS4); the readout is intrinsic to the muscle and its microvasculature, not an inter-organ metabolic signal. Replace "directly linking" with "associating with the local metabolic–contractile machinery" or similar, to avoid implying that R1 establishes an endocrine metabolic loop.

3. **Section: Introduction, paragraph 2.** The paragraph lists several criteria for "any candidate myokine" and credits them to [R54, R55, R60]. The four-criterion list presented in the later section ("Expression in skeletal muscle: is THBS4 a bona fide myokine?") is the author's own synthesis rather than an explicit checklist from Hah et al. [R54]. To avoid implying that this is field consensus, the Introduction should attribute the operational criteria to "the present review's synthesis of recent endocrine criteria" or cite the specific reviews that articulate each criterion (e.g., [R54] for regulated secretion; [R57] for EV packaging; [R55] for systemic metabolic effects).

4. **Section: "Expression in skeletal muscle: is THBS4 a bona fide myokine?"** The criterion (i) section states that "Frolova and colleagues demonstrated that TSP-4 protein is enriched in the microvasculature of oxidative muscle and **at high levels in the medial soleus** [R1]." The R1 abstract supplied does not specify "medial soleus"; it refers to "oxidative red muscle" generally. Either verify this anatomical detail from the full text of Frolova et al. 2014 (Matrix Biol) and cite the relevant figure/panel, or soften to "in oxidative compartments of the soleus." Similar attention is warranted for "Arber and colleagues reported... that in adult skeletal muscle THBS4 is produced by interstitial cells and accumulates conspicuously at the neuromuscular junction (NMJ), where it was originally identified as a denervation-responsive transcript [R3]." R3's supplied abstract notes interstitial cell production and NMJ accumulation, but the "denervation-responsive" framing should be cross-checked against the full text — this is not in the supplied abstract.

5. **Missing concept: satellite cells and muscle regeneration.** Across ~10,000 words there is essentially no engagement with THBS4 in muscle stem cells (satellite cells), muscle regeneration after injury, or its relationship to myogenic regulatory factors (MyoD, Myogenin, Pax7). Given that the review is positioned at "the crossroads of muscle biology," this is a notable omission. Even if direct evidence is scarce, the manuscript should explicitly flag this as an open question and discuss whether the myofiber-overexpression rescue in [R2], the NMJ accumulation in [R3], and the integrin/dystroglycan trafficking mechanism [R2] generate testable predictions for satellite-cell adhesion, proliferation, or fusion.

6. **Missing concept: training modality and dose–response.** THBS4 induction has only been interrogated transcriptomically in a single eccentric-vs-concentric protocol [R5]. The review does not discuss resistance vs endurance training, intensity-dependent thresholds, or whether repeated bouts (training) versus a single bout produce different kinetics. Given that the central thesis hinges on exercise-induced THBS4 release, this is a significant gap; the Open Questions section should explicitly call for studies across exercise modalities and doses.

7. **Section: "Open questions and experimental roadmap."** The call for "an inducible, muscle-restricted Cre driver crossed to a floxed Thbs4 allele" should specify which existing tools are appropriate (e.g., HSA-MerCreMer, ACTA1-Cre-ER, MCK-Cre) and acknowledge their known caveats (leakiness, off-target recombination, incomplete myofiber coverage). Without such grounding the recommendation reads as aspirational rather than actionable.

8. **Section: "Cardiovascular THBS4."** The extrapolation that "the same mechanism plausibly explains why Thbs4-/- myofibres exhibit spontaneous dystrophic changes with age [R2]" from the cardiac stretch-mediated contractility data [R36] is speculative and should be flagged as such. Slow-force-response augmentation in cardiomyocytes and integrin/dystroglycan trafficking in myofibres are mechanistically distinct processes; the manuscript should state that the cardiac-to-skeletal-muscle extrapolation is *hypothesis-generating* rather than evidence-based.

## Minor comments

1. The abbreviation "THBS4" and "TSP-4" are used interchangeably throughout; pick one for primary use and define the alias at first occurrence.

2. The Introduction's phrase "genetic ablation accelerates dystrophic change with age, while overexpression is protective in mouse models of muscular dystrophy [R2]" is supported by [R2], but the more recent Zarén et al. study [R24] — which shows no protection in β-sarcoglycan- or laminin-α2-deficient backgrounds — should be cross-referenced even in the Introduction to forestall an overly optimistic framing.

3. The statement that THBS4 is "rapidly induced by cardiac overload and by angiotensin II, and loss-of-function studies in mice consistently reveal maladaptive remodelling" is softened later in the manuscript but the word "consistently" in the Introduction is unsupported by [R21] (lentiviral knockdown *worsens* aneurysm, opposite direction in another model).

4. The review cites the cardio-metabolic db/db study [R14] as evidence for "fibroblast-specific disruption of TbR2 or Smad3 attenuates fibrosis, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and contractile dysfunction" — accurate, but the manuscript should note that this is *cardiac* fibroblast THBS4, not skeletal-muscle-resident fibroblast THBS4, and the relevance to skeletal muscle is not established.

5. In the section on soluble vs EV-associated THBS4, the manuscript calls for "density-gradient EV isolation with immune-depletion strategies anchored by muscle-restricted tagged alleles." This is technically demanding; briefly note that current EV isolation methods (e.g., iodixanol, SEC) cannot fully separate THBS4-carrying EVs from THBS4-coated vesicles or soluble pentamer co-sedimenting in EV fractions.

6. The "Mass photometry and electron microscopy" recommendation in the Open Questions section for verifying recombinant pentamer is appropriate, but the manuscript should also note that cryo-EM of the pentamer in complex with putative receptors has not been reported.

7. The claim "human, but not Xenopus, THBS4 carries an RGD motif in the third type-3 repeat [R40, R41]" — this is consistent with R40 (Lawler et al. 1995) and R41 (Lawler et al. 1993), but the mechanism by which the Xenopus orthologue engages integrins without an RGD is not discussed; briefly note that α7β1 coupling may involve non-RGD-dependent binding sites if known.

8. The Cingolani et al. [R36] citation for "stretch-mediated contractility augmentation" is accurate, but the manuscript should clarify that this is the *slow force response* — a specific cardiomyocyte phenomenon — and not conflate it with skeletal-muscle stretch-activated contraction.

9. The discussion of TWEAK/Fn14 in [R44] is acknowledged briefly, but TWEAK is a known regulator of skeletal muscle atrophy and the manuscript misses an opportunity to position THBS4 within the broader atrophy/hypertrophy regulatory network beyond THBS1 [R17].

10. The Risk of cancer framing (HCC [R29], CRC [R27], gastric [R33], breast [R51]) is appropriately flagged in Critical Perspectives. However, the manuscript should explicitly note that THBS4 SNPs in [R33] showed only "modest" associations and that several of the cancer studies are correlative rather than mechanistic, lest the therapeutic-extrapolation warnings appear stronger than the underlying evidence warrants.

11. The conclusion "Every other metabolic or inflammatory readout…is anchored in tissues that abundantly express THBS4 themselves" is a useful framing; consider adding that this *also* means THBS4 is a candidate biomarker of tissue stress across multiple organs, even if its endocrine credentials remain unproven.

12. Consider adding a sentence on species differences: zebrafish Tsp4b is essential for MTJ attachment [R4], but Thbs4-null mice do not exhibit frank muscle detachment — only dystrophic changes with ageing [R2]. This species gap deserves explicit comment rather than implicit dismissal.

13. The statement "TGF-β is also a major driver of THBS4 induction in db/db diabetic cardiomyopathy [R14] and in endothelial cells [R10]" is accurate; the manuscript could note that this creates a circular regulatory motif (THBS4 amplifies TGF-β signalling; TGF-β induces THBS4), which has implications for therapeutic targeting.

14. Minor stylistic: the phrase "credentialed matricellular muscle-enriched factor with strong endocrine potential" in the Introduction is unusual; rephrase to "well-characterised matricellular, muscle-enriched factor with candidate endocrine potential" for clarity.

15. The exact cohort size for the Yang et al. [R49] Alzheimer's study is given as "small (n = 39)" — verify this against the full paper; the supplied abstract does not give n, only describing the population as "asymptomatic individuals from families with autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease."

# Reviewer 2 — ageing, metabolism & translation

## Summary assessment

This is a substantive, well-organized review that competently synthesizes the rapidly expanding THBS4 literature across muscle, heart, ageing and metabolism. The author rightly resists prematurely promoting THBS4 to bona fide myokine status and correctly emphasizes context-dependence. However, the manuscript is undermined by an uncritical framing of the "rejuvenating factor" hypothesis (built largely on a single in vitro study [R47]), incomplete integration of the pro-tumorigenic THBS4 literature with the rejuvenation narrative, absence of sex-specific analysis, and insufficient quantitative anchoring for clinical translation. **Recommendation: major revision** before acceptance, with particular attention to tightening the causal claims, adding sex/age stratification and effect-size estimates, and balancing the rejuvenation and oncological perspectives.

---

## Major comments

**1. Introduction, paragraph 3** — *"…the loss of skeletal muscle mass and function with age (sarcopenia) and the parallel decline in muscle quality and metabolic flexibility represent one of the strongest predictors of morbidity and mortality in older adults [R55, R58]."*
**Problem:** R55 is a clinical sports nutrition primer; R58 is a muscle–brain axis narrative review. Neither card excerpt establishes the strong-predictor epidemiological claim. The citation is therefore miscast.
**Fix:** Replace with a primary epidemiology citation (e.g., landmark cohort studies on grip strength, muscle mass and all-cause mortality) and remove the unsupported strength claim, or weaken to "an important correlate of adverse outcomes."

**2. "Expression in skeletal muscle" section, opening paragraph** — *"'bona fide myokine' advanced in recent endocrine reviews requires… (i) expressed in myofibers… (ii) secreted in a regulated fashion… (iii) detectable in the systemic circulation… (iv) capable of exerting bioactivity on distant target tissues through defined receptor pathways [R54, R55, R60]."*
**Problem:** This four-criterion operational definition is not identical to the field-standard Febbraio–Pedersen criteria (which require stimulus-secretion coupling, circulating protein quantification with arteriovenous gradients, and receptor identification on a distal target). Presenting it as "advanced in recent endocrine reviews" without naming the foundational sources weakens methodological transparency.
**Fix:** Acknowledge explicitly that the definition re-frames the classical Febbraio–Pedersen criteria, cite the original sources, and note where the criteria have been refined for the EV era.

**3. "THBS4 protein structure" section, paragraph 1** — *"…electron microscopy resolves five globular 'arms' radiating from a central assembly domain [R40].… rotary-shadowed images of bovine tendon TSP-4… [R45]"*
**Problem:** Card R45 specifies "Electron microscopy of this sample" without naming rotary shadowing; attributing this specific EM modality to that paper risks fabricating a method that may belong to adjacent studies.
**Fix:** Use the generic "electron microscopy of purified bovine tendon TSP-4" and remove "rotary-shadowed" unless a primary source can be provided.

**4. Introduction and Ageing section — Gan rejuvenation narrative** — *"the cornerstone observation… is that THBS4 protein is enriched in serum from young but not old mice, and that recombinant THBS4 applied directly to transdifferentiated human neurons roughly doubled synapse numbers…"*
**Problem:** Gan et al. [R47] is an in vitro neuronal assay with recombinant protein; it is not in vivo evidence that endogenous circulating THBS4 mediates the parabiosis or cognition effects. Presenting this as the "cornerstone" elevates the hypothesis beyond what the card supports. The Introduction later concedes the limitation, but the rhetorical placement is misleading.
**Fix:** Demote the language from "cornerstone" to "a key in vitro observation awaiting in vivo validation"; move the limitations paragraph out of the conclusion into the main text where this claim is first introduced.

**5. "Cardiovascular THBS4" section, paragraph on electrophysiology** — *"Exogenous TSP-4 prolongs action potential duration… whether a parallel mechanism operates at the sarcolemma or transverse tubules of skeletal muscle is unknown."*
**Problem:** The manuscript extrapolates extensively from cardiac to skeletal muscle THBS4 biology throughout this section (stretch-mediated contractility, mechano-transduction, fibroblast states). The explicit acknowledgement that Kv/L-type Ca²⁺ effects have no skeletal muscle analogue undercuts the broader extrapolation strategy; the section should consistently flag inference vs. demonstrated mechanism.
**Fix:** Add a single explicit paragraph separating demonstrated cardiac mechanisms from skeletal muscle inferences, and use language such as "if extrapolated to skeletal muscle" rather than "in skeletal muscle."

**6. "Critical perspectives and controversies" section — cancer integration** — *"Any therapeutic strategy aimed at boosting circulating THBS4 for muscle or cognitive benefit must therefore contend with its tumour-promoting potential, a tension that is rarely acknowledged in the 'rejuvenating factor' narrative."*
**Problem:** The manuscript acknowledges the paradox in a single paragraph but never integrates it into the central thesis. A "rejuvenating factor" that is independently prognostic for shortened survival in HCC [R29], drives PDGFRβ-dependent CRC invasion [R27], and fuels HER2⁺ mammary tumour progression [R51] is, a priori, implausible as a systemic therapeutic target. This deserves an early, prominent framing alongside the sarcopenia/rejuvenation argument.
**Fix:** Move the cancer paradox into the Introduction's "central question" framing and add a brief quantitative note (e.g., HR for HCC survival in [R29]) to anchor clinical risk.

**7. Whole manuscript** — **Sex as a biological variable is absent.**
**Problem:** Skeletal muscle myokine biology, sarcopenia progression, and cardiovascular remodelling all show substantial sexual dimorphism. None of the cited cards (R1, R2, R30, R35, R43) report sex-stratified analyses, and the manuscript does not flag this gap. Translational recommendations therefore implicitly assume sex-neutral biology.
**Fix:** Add an "Open questions" bullet mandating sex-stratified analysis in conditional knockouts and human cohorts, and note where the cited studies used mixed-sex vs. single-sex designs.

**8. "Systemic metabolism and inter-organ crosstalk" section** — **No coverage of insulin resistance, NAFLD, or glycaemic control.**
**Problem:** The manuscript's stated focus is "ageing, metabolism & translation," but metabolic coverage is limited to local LPL/VLDL handling [R1], macrophage-driven vascular inflammation [R31], and diabetic cardiac fibrosis [R14]. Insulin sensitivity, hepatic glucose output, and exercise-induced glucose uptake — central endpoints for any myokine candidacy — are not addressed.
**Fix:** Add a paragraph addressing whether conditional Thbs4 deletion in muscle alters whole-body insulin tolerance, hepatic triglyceride content, or glucose infusion rates in clamp studies; flag the absence of such experiments as a critical gap.

**9. "Open questions and experimental roadmap" section** — **No quantitative effect-size targets.**
**Problem:** The roadmap calls for "deeply phenotyped human cohorts" and "authentic pentameric recombinant THBS4" but does not specify the minimum effect size that would constitute clinical relevance (e.g., what fold-change in plasma THBS4 between young and old would predict frailty with OR > 2). Without such anchoring, the experimental priorities remain aspirational.
**Fix:** Add explicit quantitative thresholds derived from the existing human data — e.g., the Yang [R49] effect size, the Gan [R47] young/old serum ratio, and the pulmonary hypertension serum elevations in [R6, R7] — as benchmarks for future studies.

**10. Whole manuscript — Causality/association grading** — **No formal assessment of evidence strength.**
**Problem:** The review repeatedly distinguishes "association" from "causation" in qualitative terms, but provides no systematic framework (Bradford Hill, GRADE, or equivalent) for ranking the THBS4–sarcopenia and THBS4–cognition links. The Yang Alzheimer's study is acknowledged as small (n = 39, per the manuscript's own claim), but no quantitative confidence interval is reported.
**Fix:** Add a short methods-style note on evidence grading and apply it to the three strongest translational claims (THBS4 as rejuvenating factor; THBS4 as metabolic regulator; THBS4 as aneurysm-protective).

**11. "Molecular mechanisms II" section, paragraph on EVs** — *"[R54, R57]… exerkines are delivered in distinct vesicular and non-vesicular pools."*
**Problem:** No card provides evidence that THBS4 is packaged into or trafficked via EVs; the EV framing is therefore imported from the myokine field rather than demonstrated for THBS4. The manuscript itself acknowledges this, but the language can give readers the opposite impression.
**Fix:** Add explicit "no primary evidence links THBS4 to EV cargo to date" and remove the phrase "exerkines are delivered in distinct vesicular and non-vesicular pools" from sentences discussing THBS4 specifically.

**12. "Critical perspectives" section, fibrotic literature paragraph** — *"[R23] chronic kidney disease, where serum levels rise with declining eGFR and knockdown attenuates fibrosis in the UUO model."*
**Problem:** The CKD study [R23] is described alongside cardiac and tumour literature to argue that THBS4 "amplifies whatever TGF-β1/Smad… programme is already running." This is a tidy synthesis but risks over-generalising across tissues with very different microenvironments. The pro-fibrotic vs. anti-fibrotic reconciliation is asserted rather than tested.
**Fix:** Add a sentence acknowledging that this reconciliation is a working hypothesis, and that no study has directly compared TGF-β tone across these tissues to test the model.

# Reviewer 3 — editor, methods & citation integrity

## Summary assessment

This is a substantial, critically-framed review that performs a useful service by foregrounding the gap between THBS4's status as a credentialed matricellular muscle factor and its under-supported candidacy as a bona fide myokine. The narrative architecture — structure, expression, two molecular-mechanism chapters, ageing, metabolism, cardiac extrapolation, critical perspectives, roadmap — is coherent and the prose is generally clear. However, the manuscript contains at least one fabricated "paradox" in the AAA literature, a citation error on a key mechanistic claim, an internal contradiction about THBS4 as "anti-fibrotic," and a misrepresentation of the Willis et al. exercise study design. **Recommendation: major revisions** before the manuscript is acceptable for the cited evidence base.

## Major comments

1. **Fabricated "paradox" in the angiotensin II/AAA literature (Critical perspectives section).** The passage: *"Yet the same loss of Thbs4 in an angiotensin II-induced abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) model is vasoprotective, because here TSP-4 is induced as an adaptive response and its knockdown accelerates aneurysm progression with increased inflammation [R21]"* is internally incoherent and contradicted by its own cited evidence. R21 explicitly shows that *lentiviral Thbs4 knockdown accelerates aneurysm progression* — i.e., Thbs4 loss is *harmful*, not vasoprotective, in this model. The parallel R30 and R32 studies likewise show that global Thbs4 KO on an Ang II background *worsens* cardiac and aortic pathology. All three cited cards converge on loss-of-Thbs4 being detrimental; there is no "reconciliation" required. The author must either remove the false paradox or supply a genuine one (e.g., the Thbs4 KO *baseline* aortic phenotype versus the *induced* AAA phenotype in R21, or the difference between constitutive KO and acute lentiviral knockdown).

2. **Citation misattribution for the integrin/dystroglycan trafficking claim (Cardiovascular THBS4 section).** The sentence *"if THBS4 is required for stress-induced trafficking of integrin and dystroglycan attachment complexes in cardiomyocytes [R9]…"* is wrong on two counts: R9 describes a secretion-defective Thbs4 mutant causing sarcolemmal rupture, not integrin/dystroglycan trafficking; and the integrin/dystroglycan trafficking finding is from skeletal muscle in R2 (Vanhoutte et al., 2016). Cite R2 (or R2 + the supporting R15) and explicitly note the extrapolation from skeletal to cardiac muscle.

3. **Internal contradiction on fibrosis (Introduction vs. Critical perspectives).** The Introduction presents THBS4's signature feature as "*promoting rather than inhibiting angiogenesis, attenuating rather than driving fibrosis*" (cited to R8). This is contradicted by R14 (THBS4 upregulated in TGF-β-driven fibroblasts in db/db hearts, with fibroblast-specific TbR2/Smad3 loss attenuating fibrosis), R18 (the Fibroblast-Thbs4 cluster *promotes* fibrosis), R23 (THBS4 pro-fibrotic in CKD, knockdown attenuates UUO fibrosis), R22 (THBS4 supports trophoblast invasion via TGF-β1/Smad), and R51 (THBS4 fuels HER2+ tumour progression). The Critical perspectives section correctly identifies this dichotomy; the Introduction must do the same and not present THBS4 as a uniform anti-fibrotic agent.

4. **Misrepresentation of the Willis et al. study design (Introduction, Expression in skeletal muscle, Open questions).** The manuscript repeatedly characterises R5 as demonstrating THBS4 upregulation "*after a single bout of untrained eccentric exercise*" — for example, in the Introduction: "*THBS4 emerged as a predicted 'hub' within a secretome module upregulated after untrained eccentric exercise.*" R5 is titled *"Transcriptomic adaptation during skeletal muscle habituation to eccentric or concentric exercise training"* and analyses muscle over a **2-week eccentric-vs-concentric running protocol**. The THBS4 signal is a network-level prediction from this training study, not a single-bout response. Revise every reference to the study's design and clarify that "untrained" refers to the early/habituation phase of training rather than a single acute bout.

5. **"Defective collagen maturation identified as the primary driver" attributed to R35 (Cardiovascular THBS4 section).** The R35 abstract (Frolova et al., FASEB J 2012) reports greater heart weight and increased interstitial collagen deposition in Thbs4-/- TAC mice, but does not itself identify "defective collagen maturation" as the primary driver — that mechanistic language comes from the R44 review (Mustonen et al., Ann Med 2012), which cites R35. Either rephrase to match R35's actual conclusions or cite R44 for the mechanistic interpretation, and avoid the "primary driver" framing in the absence of direct experimental support.

## Minor comments

1. **"Three N-glycosylation sequons mapped in the zebrafish orthologue [R39]."** This specific quantitative claim is not visible in the (truncated) R39 card text and should be either softened ("predicted N-glycosylation sites") or accompanied by a precise figure citation if the full paper supports it.

2. **"T3R and TSP-C domains of THBS4 are both necessary and sufficient for interactions with BiP (Grp78) and ATF6α [R11]."** R11 states these domains are "*required*", which is consistent with necessity but not sufficiency. Use "*necessary*" (or "*required*") and drop "*and sufficient*" unless directly supported.

3. **"The LamG domain localises to the Golgi [R11]."** A LamG domain is not listed among THBS4's canonical sub-groups (N-terminal / type-2 EGF / type-3 Ca²⁺-binding / C-terminal) in either R25 or R41, and the visible R11 card does not mention a LamG domain. Either clarify which domain is meant or remove the sentence.

4. **First-person constructions in a review manuscript.** Phrases such as *"I propose that THBS4 functions as a condition-dependent matricellular buffer"* and *"we view less as evidence of absence than as evidence that the protein has not yet been interrogated"* are unidiomatic for a single-author or multi-author review. Convert to author-neutral formulations or explicitly designate the author as a viewpoint-holder.

5. **"A credentialed matricellular muscle-enriched factor" (Introduction).** "*Credentialed*" is jargon-laden and imprecise. Replace with something like "*well-documented*" or "*established as a matricellular factor in skeletal muscle*".

6. **"WGCNA / weighted gene co-expression analysis" used to describe R5.** The visible R5 abstract refers to "*transcriptome-wide network and secretome analyses*" without specifying WGCNA. Either confirm the method from the full paper or use the more general "*network-based transcriptomic analysis*".

7. **"A Rosetta Stone for interpreting the more nascent skeletal muscle data" (Cardiovascular section).** The metaphor is informal and editorially loaded for a scientific review. Rephrase to "*a more mature paradigm that can guide interpretation of the skeletal muscle data*".

8. **Lengthy sentences that impede readability.** Examples include the multi-clause sentences in the Cardiovascular THBS4 section (e.g., the run-on summarising Palao et al.) and the second paragraph of the Molecular mechanisms I chapter ("*The most explicit mechanistic link…*"). These should be broken up for clarity.

9. **The phrase "more than two decades ago" for Arber et al. (1995)** is correct if the manuscript is dated 2025/26, but a precise date ("*since 1995*") is preferable for a review.

10. **Redundancy between the Cardiovascular and Critical perspectives sections.** The Palao/R30/R32 findings, the Fibroblast-Thbs4 concept, and the matrifibrocyte framing are each discussed in both sections. Consolidate to avoid repetition.

11. **"…or undefined oligomeric states, rendering the literature difficult to integrate" (Open questions).** The phrasing slightly editorialises; "*complicating cross-study comparison*" is more neutral.

12. **The manuscript sometimes conflates "ECM" with "secreted" without acknowledging that matrix deposition is itself a form of secretion.** When discussing why THBS4 "must transit through the secretory pathway" (Molecular mechanisms I), note explicitly that even matrix-anchored THBS4 is, by definition, a secreted glycoprotein — the salient point is *productive ER-to-Golgi trafficking and extracellular retention*, not secretion per se.

13. **"Recombinant human THBS4 assembles into a 550 kDa multimer composed of 140 kDa subunits when expressed in C2C12 skeletal muscle myoblasts [R40]"** is correct, but the protein structure section adds "*and electron microscopy resolves five globular 'arms' radiating from a central assembly domain [R40]*" — the rotary-shadowed EM is from R45 (bovine tendon), not R40. R40 reports the biochemical mass only. Move the EM claim to its R45 citation.
