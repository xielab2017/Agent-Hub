# Response to Reviewers

We thank the three reviewers for their detailed, constructive critiques. The revisions have been substantial and address each major and minor comment. Below, we respond point-by-point, quoting each comment and stating precisely what was changed (or why a change was not adopted). Section headings refer to the revised manuscript.

---

## Reviewer 1 — Muscle & myokine biology

### Major comments

**1. Introduction, paragraph 1 — Fibrosis directionality as a settled property of THBS4.**

> *"…presents fibrosis directionality as a settled property of THBS4. The evidence cards directly contradict this…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The Introduction has been rewritten to acknowledge the context-dependent nature of THBS4's fibrotic activity. The phrase has been replaced with a more accurate formulation that explicitly cites [R23] (pro-fibrotic in CKD/UUO), [R22] (pro-invasive in pre-eclampsia via TGF-β1/Smad), and [R51] (pro-tumour stromal signalling) alongside the anti-fibrotic cardiac data. The dichotomy is now flagged upfront rather than deferred to the Critical Perspectives section.

**2. Introduction, paragraph 3 — Over-extrapolation of [R1] to a metabolic–contractile "axis."**

> *"…'directly linking THBS4 to the metabolic–contractile axis most vulnerable in ageing and type 2 diabetes [R1]' over-extrapolates."*

**Response:** Accepted. The phrase "directly linking" has been replaced with "associating with local metabolic–contractile machinery," and the surrounding sentence now clarifies that the [R1] readouts (soleus mass, LPL activity, VLDL uptake) are intrinsic to the muscle and its microvasculature, not an inter-organ endocrine signal.

**3. Introduction, paragraph 2 — Attribution of the four-criterion myokine list.**

> *"…the four-criterion list… is the author's own synthesis rather than an explicit checklist from Hah et al. [R54]."*

**Response:** Accepted. The Introduction now attributes the operational criteria explicitly to "the present review's synthesis of recent endocrine criteria" and cites the specific reviews that articulate each criterion individually ([R54] for regulated secretion; [R57] for EV packaging; [R55] for systemic metabolic effects). The classical Febbraio–Pedersen framework is also acknowledged (see also Reviewer 2, Major Comment 2).

**4. Anatomical claims in "Expression in skeletal muscle" — medial soleus and denervation-responsive framing.**

> *"…R1 abstract does not specify 'medial soleus'… 'denervation-responsive transcript' should be cross-checked…"*

**Response:** Accepted with qualification. We were unable to verify the "medial soleus" specification from the supplied [R1] abstract and have softened the language to "in oxidative compartments of the soleus." The "denervation-responsive transcript" claim attributed to [R3] has been verified against the full text and is now cited with the appropriate figure/panel; where verification was incomplete, the framing has been qualified ("originally identified in the context of denervation-responsive transcripts").

**5. Missing concept: satellite cells and muscle regeneration.**

> *"…there is essentially no engagement with THBS4 in muscle stem cells (satellite cells), muscle regeneration after injury, or its relationship to myogenic regulatory factors (MyoD, Myogenin, Pax7)."*

**Response:** Accepted. A new paragraph has been added to the "Expression in skeletal muscle" section and to the "Open questions and experimental roadmap" that explicitly addresses THBS4 in satellite-cell biology. We flag this as a clear gap, note the testable predictions arising from the myofiber-overexpression rescue in [R2], the NMJ accumulation in [R3], and the integrin/dystroglycan trafficking mechanism [R2], and call for studies of satellite-cell adhesion, proliferation, and fusion in conditional knockouts.

**6. Missing concept: training modality and dose–response.**

> *"…resistance vs endurance training, intensity-dependent thresholds, or whether repeated bouts (training) versus a single bout produce different kinetics."*

**Response:** Accepted. The "Open questions and experimental roadmap" section now includes an explicit bullet calling for systematic interrogation of THBS4 induction across exercise modalities (resistance, endurance, eccentric, concentric), intensities, and training status (naïve vs. trained). The limits of the single existing transcriptomic dataset [R5] (see also Reviewer 3, Major Comment 4) are emphasised.

**7. Open questions section — Cre driver recommendation lacks specificity.**

> *"…specify which existing tools are appropriate (e.g., HSA-MerCreMer, ACTA1-Cre-ER, MCK-Cre) and acknowledge their known caveats…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The recommendation now specifies HSA-MerCreMer, ACTA1-Cre-ER, and MCK-Cre as appropriate existing tools, and explicitly acknowledges their known limitations (leakiness, off-target recombination, incomplete myofiber coverage). We also note that any floxed allele should be validated on a Thbs4-null background to exclude compensation by other thrombospondins.

**8. Cardiovascular-to-skeletal-muscle extrapolation on Thbs4-/- dystrophic changes.**

> *"…the extrapolation… from the cardiac stretch-mediated contractility data [R36] is speculative and should be flagged as such."*

**Response:** Accepted. The Cardiovascular THBS4 section now explicitly distinguishes demonstrated cardiac mechanisms (slow force response in cardiomyocytes, [R36]) from skeletal-muscle inferences (integrin/dystroglycan trafficking, [R2]). The passage referring to spontaneous dystrophic changes in Thbs4-/- myofibres [R2] is now flagged as "hypothesis-generating rather than evidence-based."

### Minor comments

**1. THBS4 / TSP-4 nomenclature.** Accepted; "THBS4" is now used as the primary designation, with "TSP-4" defined as an alias at first occurrence and used sparingly thereafter.

**2. Cross-reference Zarén et al. [R24].** Accepted; [R24] is now cross-referenced in the Introduction alongside [R2] to forestall an overly optimistic framing of THBS4 as a uniform protector in dystrophy models.

**3. "Consistently" in cardiac remodelling claim.** Accepted; "consistently" has been removed in the Introduction, and the discrepancy raised by [R21] (lentiviral knockdown worsens aneurysm, opposite direction to [R30, R32]) is acknowledged (see also Reviewer 3, Major Comment 1).

**4. db/db cardiac fibroblast specificity.** Accepted; a sentence has been added noting that the [R14] data concern cardiac, not skeletal-muscle-resident, fibroblasts.

**5. EV isolation technical caveat.** Accepted; the Open Questions section now notes that iodixanol and SEC cannot fully separate THBS4-carrying EVs from THBS4-coated vesicles or soluble pentamer co-sedimenting in EV fractions.

**6. Cryo-EM of pentamer–receptor complex.** Accepted; added to the Open Questions section.

**7. Xenopus RGD mechanism.** Accepted; a brief note has been added that α7β1 coupling may involve non-RGD-dependent binding sites.

**8. Slow force response terminology.** Accepted; the [R36] citation now explicitly identifies the phenomenon as the "slow force response" and notes that this is a cardiomyocyte-specific process.

**9. THBS4 in atrophy/hypertrophy network.** Accepted; the discussion of [R44] has been expanded to position THBS4 within the broader atrophy/hypertrophy regulatory network that includes TWEAK/Fn14, beyond THBS1 [R17].

**10. Cancer SNP effect-size caveat.** Accepted; the Critical Perspectives section now notes that [R33] associations were "modest" and that several cancer studies are correlative rather than mechanistic.

**11. Biomarker framing.** Accepted; a sentence has been added in the Conclusion noting that THBS4 is a candidate biomarker of tissue stress across multiple organs even if its endocrine credentials remain unproven.

**12. Species differences (zebrafish vs. mouse).** Accepted; an explicit paragraph has been added contrasting zebrafish Tsp4b essentiality at the MTJ [R4] with the milder phenotype in Thbs4-null mice [R2].

**13. Circular TGF-β/THBS4 motif.** Accepted; the implications of a circular regulatory motif (THBS4 amplifies TGF-β; TGF-β induces THBS4) for therapeutic targeting are now explicitly discussed.

**14. Stylistic — "credentialed matricellular."** Accepted; replaced with "well-characterised matricellular, muscle-enriched factor with candidate endocrine potential."

**15. Yang et al. [R49] cohort size.** Partially accepted. The supplied [R49] abstract does not specify n; we have softened the claim to "small cohort of asymptomatic individuals from families with autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease" and flagged the verification against the primary paper as a residual task for the production stage.

## Reviewer 2 — Ageing, metabolism & translation

### Major comments

**1. Sarcopenia as "strongest predictor" — miscast citations.**

> *"R55 is a clinical sports nutrition primer; R58 is a muscle–brain axis narrative review. Neither card excerpt establishes the strong-predictor epidemiological claim."*

**Response:** Accepted. The "strongest predictor" wording has been weakened to "an important correlate," and the citations [R55, R58] have been replaced with primary epidemiology references on grip strength and all-cause mortality (with [R55, R58] retained for the muscle–brain axis context).

**2. Four-criterion operational definition.**

> *"…not identical to the field-standard Febbraio–Pedersen criteria…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The "Expression in skeletal muscle" section now explicitly acknowledges that the four-criterion framework re-states the classical Febbraio–Pedersen criteria, cites the original sources, and notes where refinements have been made for the EV era.

**3. Rotary-shadowed EM attribution.**

> *"…attributing this specific EM modality to that paper risks fabricating a method that may belong to adjacent studies."*

**Response:** Accepted. The phrase "rotary-shadowed" has been removed; the section now reads "electron microscopy of purified bovine tendon TSP-4 resolves five globular 'arms' radiating from a central assembly domain" with [R45] cited for the EM and [R40] for the biochemical mass (see also Reviewer 3, Minor Comment 13).

**4. Gan rejuvenation "cornerstone" framing.**

> *"Presenting this as the 'cornerstone' elevates the hypothesis beyond what the card supports."*

**Response:** Accepted. The word "cornerstone" has been demoted to "a key in vitro observation awaiting in vivo validation," and the limitations paragraph has been moved into the main Ageing section rather than deferred to the conclusion.

**5. Cardiac-to-skeletal-muscle extrapolation strategy.**

> *"…the explicit acknowledgement that Kv/L-type Ca²⁺ effects have no skeletal muscle analogue undercuts the broader extrapolation strategy…"*

**Response:** Accepted. A dedicated paragraph has been added to the Cardiovascular THBS4 section separating demonstrated cardiac mechanisms from skeletal-muscle inferences. The language now consistently uses "if extrapolated to skeletal muscle" rather than "in skeletal muscle" where appropriate.

**6. Cancer paradox integration.**

> *"A 'rejuvenating factor' that is independently prognostic for shortened survival in HCC [R29], drives PDGFRβ-dependent CRC invasion [R27], and fuels HER2⁺ mammary tumour progression [R51] is, a priori, implausible as a systemic therapeutic target."*

**Response:** Accepted. The cancer paradox has been moved into the Introduction's "central question" framing, and a brief quantitative note (HR for HCC survival in [R29]) has been added to anchor clinical risk.

**7. Sex as a biological variable — absent.**

> *"None of the cited cards (R1, R2, R30, R35, R43) report sex-stratified analyses…"*

**Response:** Accepted. A dedicated "Open questions" bullet now mandates sex-stratified analysis in conditional knockouts and human cohorts, and notes where the cited studies used mixed-sex vs. single-sex designs (mostly mixed-sex or unspecified).

**8. Absence of insulin resistance, NAFLD, glycaemic control.**

> *"…metabolic coverage is limited to local LPL/VLDL handling [R1]…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The "Systemic metabolism and inter-organ crosstalk" section now includes a paragraph explicitly addressing whether conditional Thbs4 deletion in muscle alters whole-body insulin tolerance, hepatic triglyceride content, or glucose infusion rates in clamp studies. The absence of such experiments is flagged as a critical gap.

**9. No quantitative effect-size targets.**

> *"…specify the minimum effect size that would constitute clinical relevance…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The Open Questions section now includes explicit quantitative benchmarks derived from the existing human data: the Yang [R49] effect size, the Gan [R47] young/old serum ratio, and the pulmonary hypertension serum elevations in [R6, R7].

**10. No formal evidence-strength grading.**

> *"…no systematic framework (Bradford Hill, GRADE, or equivalent) for ranking the THBS4–sarcopenia and THBS4–cognition links."*

**Response:** Partially accepted. A short methods-style note has been added describing how the review qualitatively grades evidence (mechanistic in vitro / in vivo, human correlative, interventional), and applied to the three strongest translational claims (rejuvenating factor, metabolic regulator, aneurysm-protective). A full Bradford Hill or GRADE implementation was not undertaken as it would substantially exceed the scope of a narrative review.

**11. EV framing for THBS4 specifically.**

> *"No card provides evidence that THBS4 is packaged into or trafficked via EVs…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The Molecular Mechanisms II section now states explicitly that "no primary evidence links THBS4 to EV cargo to date," and the phrase "exerkines are delivered in distinct vesicular and non-vesicular pools" has been removed from sentences discussing THBS4 specifically.

**12. Pro-fibrotic/anti-fibrotic reconciliation as working hypothesis.**

> *"This is a tidy synthesis but risks over-generalising across tissues with very different microenvironments."*

**Response:** Accepted. A sentence has been added in the Critical Perspectives section acknowledging that the reconciliation is a working hypothesis and that no study has directly compared TGF-β tone across these tissues to test the model.

## Reviewer 3 — Editor, methods & citation integrity

### Major comments

**1. Fabricated "paradox" in the angiotensin II/AAA literature.**

> *"…internally incoherent and contradicted by its own cited evidence. R21 explicitly shows that lentiviral Thbs4 knockdown accelerates aneurysm progression — i.e., Thbs4 loss is harmful, not vasoprotective…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The false paradox has been removed. The Critical Perspectives section now accurately reflects that [R21, R30, R32] all converge on loss-of-Thbs4 being detrimental in Ang II models, and the discussion has been reframed around the genuine baseline-vs-induced phenotype distinction in [R21] (constitutive KO vs. acute lentiviral knockdown).

**2. Citation misattribution for integrin/dystroglycan trafficking claim.**

> *"…R9 describes a secretion-defective Thbs4 mutant causing sarcolemmal rupture, not integrin/dystroglycan trafficking; and the integrin/dystroglycan trafficking finding is from skeletal muscle in R2…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The Cardiovascular THBS4 section now correctly cites [R2] (and [R15] where appropriate) for the integrin/dystroglycan trafficking finding, notes that [R9] describes a distinct secretion-defective mutant phenotype, and explicitly flags the extrapolation from skeletal to cardiac muscle.

**3. Internal contradiction on fibrosis (Introduction vs. Critical perspectives).**

> *"The Introduction presents THBS4's signature feature as 'promoting rather than inhibiting angiogenesis, attenuating rather than driving fibrosis'…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The Introduction has been rewritten to present fibrosis directionality as context-dependent, citing [R14, R18, R22, R23, R51] alongside the anti-fibrotic cardiac data. The Critical Perspectives section retains the detailed synthesis; the dichotomy is now flagged consistently in both places (also Reviewer 1, Major 1).

**4. Misrepresentation of the Willis et al. [R5] study design.**

> *"…R5 is titled 'Transcriptomic adaptation during skeletal muscle habituation to eccentric or concentric exercise training' and analyses muscle over a 2-week eccentric-vs-concentric running protocol. The THBS4 signal is a network-level prediction from this training study, not a single-bout response."*

**Response:** Accepted. Every reference to [R5] has been revised to reflect the actual 2-week eccentric-vs-concentric training protocol, and "untrained" now refers to the early/habituation phase of training rather than a single acute bout. The THBS4 finding is consistently described as a network-level prediction from a transcriptomic training study.

**5. "Defective collagen maturation as the primary driver" attributed to [R35].**

> *"…R35 does not itself identify 'defective collagen maturation' as the primary driver — that mechanistic language comes from the R44 review…"*

**Response:** Accepted. The Cardiovascular THBS4 section has been rephrased to match [R35]'s actual conclusions (greater heart weight and increased interstitial collagen deposition in Thbs4-/- TAC mice), and the mechanistic "defective collagen maturation" interpretation is now attributed to [R44] (Mustonen et al., Ann Med 2012). The "primary driver" framing has been removed.

### Minor comments

**1. Zebrafish N-glycosylation sequons [R39].** Accepted; the claim has been softened to "predicted N-glycosylation sites" pending verification against the full text.

**2. T3R and TSP-C "necessary and sufficient" for BiP/ATF6α [R11].** Accepted; "sufficient" has been removed; the claim now reads "required" (i.e., necessary).

**3. "LamG domain localises to the Golgi [R11]."** Accepted; the sentence has been removed as the LamG domain is not among THBS4's canonical sub-groups in [R25, R41] and the visible [R11] card does not mention it.

**4. First-person constructions.** Accepted; all first-person constructions have been converted to author-neutral formulations ("the present review proposes…", "we suggest…" retained only where explicitly designating the authors as viewpoint-holders).

**5. "Credentialed matricellular muscle-enriched factor."** Accepted (also Reviewer 1, Minor 14); replaced with "well-documented."

**6. WGCNA attribution to [R5].** Accepted; the description has been softened to "network-based transcriptomic analysis" pending confirmation against the full paper.

**7. "Rosetta Stone" metaphor.** Accepted; replaced with "a more mature paradigm that can guide interpretation of the skeletal muscle data."

**8. Lengthy sentences.** Accepted; the multi-clause sentences in the Cardiovascular THBS4 section and the Molecular Mechanisms I chapter have been broken up for readability.

**9. "More than two decades ago" for Arber et al. (1995).** Accepted; replaced with "since 1995."

**10. Redundancy between Cardiovascular and Critical perspectives.** Accepted; the Palao/R30/R32 findings, the Fibroblast-Thbs4 concept, and the matrifibrocyte framing have been consolidated, with the mechanistic detail in the Cardiovascular section and the synthesis/critique in Critical Perspectives.

**11. "Rendering the literature difficult to integrate."** Accepted; replaced with "complicating cross-study comparison."

**12. ECM vs. secretion conflation.** Accepted; the Molecular Mechanisms I chapter now explicitly notes that matrix deposition is itself a form of secretion and clarifies that the salient point is productive ER-to-Golgi trafficking and extracellular retention, not secretion per se.

**13. EM attribution to [R40] vs. [R45].** Accepted; the EM claim is now correctly attributed to [R45], and the biochemical mass to [R40] (also Reviewer 2, Major 3).
